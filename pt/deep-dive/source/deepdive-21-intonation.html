🎯 Overview


What is Intonation?
Intonation is the rise and fall in the pitch of your voice while speaking. It’s not just what you say, but how you say it that affects meaning.

In English, intonation affects questions, statements, irony, politeness, emotions, and more. Unlike Brazilian Portuguese, which also uses intonation but in different patterns, English intonation is crucial for fluency and to avoid sounding robotic or unnatural.

Examples:

  1. You're coming tonight? (Rising → Question or doubt)

  2. You're coming tonight. (Falling → Statement or confirmation)

  3. Oh, really? (Depends on tone: sincere interest vs sarcasm)

🔍 Deep Dive

Main Intonation Patterns in English:

  1. Falling Intonation (↓)
    → Used in statements, commands, and wh- questions.
    Examples:

    • “He’s coming home.↓”

    • “Where are you going?↓”

  2. Rising Intonation (↑)
    → Yes/no questions, uncertainty, checking information.
    Examples:

    • “Are you ready?↑”

    • “You’re okay with that?↑”

  3. Fall-Rise Intonation (↓↑)
    → Doubts, polite corrections, surprise.
    Examples:

    • “Well, I’m not sure.↓↑”

    • “I thought you were coming.↓↑”

  4. Rise-Fall Intonation (↑↓)
    → Strong feelings: enthusiasm, sarcasm, certainty.
    Examples:

    • “Wow, that’s amazing!↑↓”

    • “Oh, great.↑↓” (can be sarcastic)

Dialogue 1 – Confirmation
– Are you done?↑
– Yes.↓
– Sure?↓↑
– Absolutely!↓

Dialogue 2 – Politeness and Doubt
– I guess… you could try that.↓↑
– I’m not sure it’ll work.↓

Dialogue 3 – Irony
– Oh, perfect.↑↓ That’s exactly what I needed. (sarcasm)

Dialogue 4 – Confusion vs Curiosity
– Really?↑ (confused)
– Reaaally.↓ (affirming)

📖 Reading Practice

“That Kind of Day”

I walked into the café, looked around, and said, “Is this seat taken?↑”
The woman looked up slowly, “No, go ahead.↓”
As I sat down, I muttered, “It’s one of those days…↓↑”
She smiled. “Tell me about it!↑↓”
I laughed, “You wouldn’t even believe what happened.↓↑”
Her eyes widened, “Try me.↑”

🔁 Deep Review

Main Points Recap:

  • Intonation = vocal pitch movement

  • Falling for statements/questions with “wh”

  • Rising for yes/no questions and uncertainty

  • Fall-rise and rise-fall for emotion, sarcasm, or politeness

  • Intonation adds emotion, meaning, and fluency
