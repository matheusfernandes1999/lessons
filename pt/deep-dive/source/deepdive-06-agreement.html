🎯 Overview

What is Agreement?
In English grammar, agreement refers to the way words change form to match each other in number, person, gender, or tense. This ensures that sentences are grammatically correct and clear.

Basic Explanation:
Agreement can occur in several forms:

  • Subject–verb agreement: The subject and verb must match in number and person.
    “She walks.” (not: “She walk”)

  • Pronoun–antecedent agreement: A pronoun must match its antecedent in number and gender.
    “Every student must bring his or her book.”

Why and When?
Agreement is crucial for coherence. Mistakes often confuse listeners/readers or create ambiguity. At C1 level, mastering agreement means navigating complex structures without errors.

🔍 Deep Dive

Agreement in English can be nuanced, especially in complex sentences. Let’s explore:

💡 Subject–Verb Agreement – Special Cases:

  • Collective Nouns:
    “The committee has decided.” (as a single unit)
    “The committee have returned to their homes.” (as individuals)

  • Indefinite Pronouns:
    “Everyone is invited.”
    “Few are aware.”

  • Either/Neither + Singular Verb:
    “Neither of the boys was late.”

💡 Pronoun–Antecedent Agreement:

  • “If a student wants to pass, he or she must study.”

    • (Formal usage; gender-neutral forms now common: “they”)

    • “If a student wants to pass, they must study.”

💡 Agreement in Complex Structures:

  • “The manager, as well as the assistants, is present.”

  • “The book or the pen is on the table.”

  • “The pens or the book is on the table.” ← Closest noun rule

🗣️ Mini Dialogues:

  1. Emma: “Everyone in the group are coming.”
    Liam: “I think you mean ‘Everyone is coming.’”

  2. Customer: “Neither the phone nor the headphones works.”
    Clerk: “Actually, it should be ‘work’ since ‘headphones’ is plural and closer.”

  3. Teacher: “Does anyone know if they left their phone?”
    (Gender-neutral singular “they” in action.)

  4. Parent: “Each of the children has their backpack.”
    (Informal, accepted modern agreement.)
