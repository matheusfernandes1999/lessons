🎯 Overview

What are Countable and Uncountable Nouns?

  • Countable nouns are things you can count: one apple, two apples.

  • Uncountable nouns cannot be separated into individual elements: milk, sugar, information.

✅ Examples:

  • Countable: book, student, idea

  • Uncountable: rice, furniture, knowledge

We use:

  • a/an, many, few with countables: a question, many books

  • much, little with uncountables: much advice, little water

🧠 Why it matters?
Portuguese and English disagree on what's countable! This can cause mistakes like:
❌ "a furniture", ❌ "informations", ❌ "many advice"

🔍 Deep Dive

🧠 Cross-language traps: English vs. Portuguese

In English (Uncountable)

Information

News

Money

Móvel (um móvel)

Furniture

Lição (lições)

Homework

Equipamento (equipamentos)

Equipment

🧩 Key rules and patterns:

  • Abstract ideas are usually uncountable in English: advice, knowledge, progress.

  • Mass nouns (you can't separate them): water, rice, flour, air.

  • Group nouns that are singular in English: furniture, luggage, hair (on head), traffic, jewelry.

✅ Correct:

  • I need some information.

  • He gave me good advice.

  • We bought some new furniture.

❌ Incorrect:

  • I need an information.

  • He gave me an advice.

  • We bought many furnitures.

👥 Real-life Dialogues

Dialogue 1:

A: Can you give me an advice?
B: You mean "some advice"! Advice is uncountable.

Dialogue 2:

A: How many homeworks do we have?
B: We don’t say “homeworks.” Just “some homework” or “a lot of homework.”

Dialogue 3:

A: She has so many hairs!
B: If you mean the hair on her head, say “a lot of hair.” "Hairs" usually means individual strands.

🔁 Deep Review

Main takeaways:

  • Some nouns that are countable in Portuguese are uncountable in English.

  • Uncountable nouns don’t use a/an or plural s.

  • Use "some," "a lot of," "a little," with uncountable nouns.
