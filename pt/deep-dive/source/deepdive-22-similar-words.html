🎯 Overview


“Similar words” can confuse learners when:

  • They sound similar (homophones).

  • They look similar (false friends).

  • They have close meanings but are used differently in context.

Examples:

  • “Accept” vs. “Except”

  • “Advice” (noun) vs. “Advise” (verb)

  • “Actually” vs. “Currently” (false friends for Brazilians)

We study them to speak and write with accuracy, avoiding awkward or incorrect choices.

🔍 Deep Dive

1. Sound-Alike Confusions

  • Accept vs. Except

    • I accept your apology.

    • Everyone except John was invited.

  • Peace vs. Piece

    • Let’s live in peace.

    • I want a piece of cake.

  • Their / There / They’re

    • Their house is big.

    • They’re going out.

    • Let’s meet there.

2. False Friends for Brazilians

  • Actually (na verdade) ≠ atualmente (currently)

  • Pretend (fingir) ≠ pretender (intend)

  • Assist (ajudar) ≠ assistir (watch)

  • Library (biblioteca) ≠ livraria (bookstore)

  • Push (empurrar) ≠ puxar (pull)

3. Similar Meaning – Different Usage

  • Big vs. Large

    • A big decision (abstract)

    • A large room (physical size)

  • Tell vs. Say

    • She told me a secret.

    • He said he was tired.

Dialogue 1:
– “Are you going to assist the meeting?”
– “You mean attend? ‘Assist’ means help.”
– “Ah, right! I always mix those two.”

Dialogue 2:
– “I want to pretend that I’m rich.”
– “Be careful! That doesn’t mean you want to plan to be rich.”

Dialogue 3:
– “He gave me a good advice.”
– “No article with ‘advice’ — it’s uncountable. Try: ‘some good advice.’”

Dialogue 4:
– “Actually, I’m living in Spain.”
– “Do you mean ‘currently’? ‘Actually’ means ‘in fact’.”

🔁 Deep Review

Key Takeaways:

  • Similar words can confuse meaning, pronunciation, or both.

  • False friends cause unique problems for Portuguese speakers.

  • Choose based on context: meaning, collocation, tone.

  • Always double-check if the word really means what you think it does.
