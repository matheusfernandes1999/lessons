🎯 Overview

What is “very”?

“Very” is an intensifier. It’s used to emphasize the degree or extent of an adjective or adverb.

Examples:

  • very cold

  • very tired

  • very quickly

Why go beyond “very”?

Using “very” repeatedly makes speech or writing basic and repetitive. At a C1 level, you need more expressive, precise vocabulary.

Instead of “very + adjective,” use:

  • Strong adjectives: freezing instead of very cold

  • Alternative intensifiers: absolutely, completely, extremely, incredibly, utterly, etc.

These sound more natural, advanced, and expressive.

🔍 Deep Dive

1. Strong Adjectives (non-gradable)

These are adjectives that already imply a strong meaning. So, “very” is unnecessary or incorrect.

Very + Adjective

Strong Adjective

very tired

exhausted

very cold

freezing

very hungry

starving

very beautiful

stunning / gorgeous

very big

enormous / gigantic

very angry

furious

very scared

terrified

very happy

thrilled / delighted

❌ Wrong: very furious, very freezing
✅ Right: absolutely furious, completely freezing

2. Alternative Intensifiers

Instead of “very,” use:

Intensifier

Use With

Example

absolutely

strong (non-gradable) adj.

absolutely furious

completely

actions or states

completely exhausted

extremely

both gradable & strong

extremely difficult

incredibly

informal, strong effect

incredibly lucky

utterly

often negative situations

utterly useless

remarkably

surprising or rare results

remarkably fast

deeply

emotions, beliefs

deeply grateful

⚠️ Be careful: You don’t say “utterly happy” — “utterly” tends to go with negative or extreme conditions.

3. Mini Dialogues

🔁 Deep Review

Summary:

  • “Very” is useful but overused and sometimes unnecessary.

  • Replace “very + adjective” with a stronger adjective or use an intensifier.

  • Some adjectives don’t need intensifiers because they are non-gradable.

  • Alternatives like “absolutely,” “utterly,” and “deeply” add richness to your speech.
