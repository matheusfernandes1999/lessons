## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** encontrar **detalhes** (números, nomes, exemplos, razões) rapidamente, sem reler o texto inteiro a cada pergunta.
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** usar palavras-chave da pergunta para ir direto ao trecho certo e confirmar a resposta com evidência.
**Tarefa verificável:** responder oito questões de detalhes com evidência textual.

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
Palavras de perguntas de detalhe — aprenda o que cada uma pede:
- **According to the passage,…** — segundo o texto (resposta explícita)
- **What is mentioned as…?** — o que é citado como…
- **Which of the following is NOT…?** — qual NÃO é… (cuidado!)
- **Why does the author mention X?** — por que o autor cita X (função do detalhe)
- **The word “X” refers to…** — a palavra X se refere a…
Palavras de texto acadêmico:
- **feature** — característica · **trait** — traço · **source** — fonte · **method** — método
- **contain** — conter · **consist of** — consistir em · **depend on** — depender de
- **approximately** — aproximadamente · **primarily** — principalmente · **rarely** — raramente
**Sinônimos que aparecem nas respostas:** *big → large/significant · help → assist/benefit · start → begin/initiate · show → demonstrate/indicate*

### Pronúncia: advérbios acadêmicos
Ouça, compare e repita: **ap-PROX-i-mate-ly · pri-MAR-i-ly · sig-NIF-i-cant-ly · RARE-ly · par-TIC-u-lar-ly**
Pares que confundem: **rarely / really** · **source / sauce / sorts** · **trait / trade / tray**
Frase: > Approximately half of the species depend primarily on a single food source.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### A técnica “pergunta → palavra-chave → trecho → paráfrase”
1. Leia a **pergunta** e sublinhe a palavra-chave mais fácil de achar (nome, número, termo técnico, palavra em maiúscula).
2. **Procure** a palavra (ou sinônimo) no texto. Não leia tudo de novo.
3. Leia **1 frase antes e 1 depois**.
4. Escolha a opção que é a **paráfrase** do trecho — não a que só repete palavras.
### Modelo
> Honeybees communicate the location of food through a movement called the “waggle dance.” When a bee returns to the hive, it moves in a figure-eight pattern. The angle of the dance indicates the direction of the food relative to the sun, while the duration of the “waggle” shows the distance. A longer waggle means the food is farther away.
**Pergunta:** *According to the passage, how do bees show distance?*
- Palavra-chave: **distance** → frase 3: *“the duration of the waggle shows the distance.”*
- Resposta paráfrase: **by how long they waggle** ✔ · armadilha: *by the angle of the dance* ✘ (isso é direção)

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: aponte a evidência
> The answer is **B** because **paragraph 2** says **“the duration of the waggle shows the distance.”**
> → C / sentence 4 / “a longer waggle means the food is farther away” → A / the first sentence / “through a movement called the waggle dance”

### 4.2 Sinônimos-relâmpago (o professor fala, o aluno responde rápido)
> large → big · begin → start · assist → help · indicate → show · purchase → buy · reside → live · require → need · obtain → get · sufficient → enough · numerous → many

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: 8 questões de detalhe (15 min)
### A. Leia — 3 min
> **Sleep and Memory**
> (1) Scientists have long known that sleep is important for health, but only in recent decades have they understood its role in memory. Sleep occurs in cycles of about 90 minutes, and each cycle includes several stages, including deep sleep and REM (rapid eye movement) sleep.
> (2) During deep sleep, the brain appears to "replay" information learned during the day. This replay helps transfer memories from the hippocampus, a temporary storage area, to the cortex, where they can be kept for a long time. Experiments with students show that those who sleep after learning a list of words remember approximately 20 to 40 percent more than those who stay awake.
> (3) REM sleep, on the other hand, seems especially important for creative problem-solving. In one study, participants who reached REM sleep during a nap were more likely to find hidden connections between ideas.
> (4) These findings have practical implications. Students who stay up all night before an exam may actually reduce their chances of remembering what they studied. Researchers recommend regular sleep schedules and short reviews before going to bed.

### B. Responda — 8 min (1 min por questão)
1. How long is one sleep cycle?
2. What does the brain do during deep sleep?
3. Where are memories stored temporarily?
4. How much more do students who sleep remember?
5. What is REM sleep especially important for?
6. What happened to nap participants who reached REM sleep?
7. According to paragraph 4, what is a risk of studying all night?
8. Which of the following is NOT recommended by researchers? a) regular sleep schedules b) short reviews before bed c) studying all night
**Acertos:** __/8
