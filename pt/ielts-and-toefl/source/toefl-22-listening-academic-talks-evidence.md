## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** em uma palestra, cada trecho tem uma **função**: afirmar uma ideia, dar um **exemplo**, dar uma **justificativa/evidência**, fazer uma **conclusão** ou uma **ressalva**. A prova pergunta muito: *“Why does the professor mention X?”*
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** ouvir um trecho e dizer para que ele serve na palestra.
**Tarefa verificável:** classificar seis trechos por função discursiva.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)
- Do you believe everything you read online? How do you know if something is true?
- Give me an opinion about your city and one reason and one example.
**Modelo de fala:** *I think my city is great for students* (opinião) *because there are many universities* (razão). *For example, there are five public universities near the center* (exemplo). *So it's a good place to study* (conclusão).
**Troque e fale:** repita o modelo trocando para: *my job / my favorite app / remote work*.
**Revisão da aula 21:** diga 4 expressões de transição e a função de cada.

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
### Funções e seus sinais
- **Afirmação (claim):** *Research suggests that… / The idea is that…*
- **Exemplo:** *For instance… / Take… / Consider the case of… / Imagine…*
- **Justificativa/evidência:** *This is because… / The reason is… / Studies show… / In one experiment…*
- **Conclusão:** *So… / Therefore… / This means that… / That's why…*
- **Ressalva (concession):** *Of course,… / Admittedly,… / That said,… / Although…*
- **Contraste:** *However… / On the other hand… / But here's the thing…*
### Palavras de pesquisa
- **experiment** · **data** · **findings** — resultados · **sample** — amostra
- **support / confirm** — sustentar / confirmar · **challenge / contradict** — questionar / contradizer
- **hypothesis** — hipótese · **correlation** — correlação · **cause** — causa

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **hypothesis** = hy-POTH-e-sis · **hypotheses** (plural) = hy-POTH-e-sees
- **data** = DAY-ta ou DAH-ta · **analysis** = a-NAL-y-sis
- **cause / course / cost** · **claim / clam / climb**
- Ênfase de contraste: *It's not the SIZE, it's the SHAPE that matters.*
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> The findings support the hypothesis, but correlation is not the same as cause.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### “Why does the professor mention X?”
A resposta quase nunca é o conteúdo de X. É **a função**: *to give an example of…, to support the idea that…, to contrast…, to show a limitation…*
### Modelo
🎧 > Many people think that being bilingual makes children confused. In fact, research suggests the opposite. In one study in Canada, bilingual children performed better on tasks that required switching attention. This is probably because they constantly choose between two languages, which trains the brain's control system. Of course, the study had a small sample, so we need more research. Still, the findings challenge the old belief.
- *“Many people think…”* → **crença comum** (vai ser contrariada)
- *“research suggests the opposite”* → **afirmação principal**
- *“In one study in Canada…”* → **evidência**
- *“This is probably because…”* → **justificativa/explicação**
- *“Of course, the study had a small sample”* → **ressalva**
- *“Still, the findings challenge…”* → **conclusão**
**Pergunta:** *Why does the professor mention the study in Canada?* → **To support the claim that bilingualism helps attention.**

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: explicando a função
> The professor mentions **the study in Canada** to **support the idea that bilingualism helps attention**.
> → the Arctic fox / give an example of camouflage → the small sample / show a limitation of the study → the old belief / contrast it with new research → traffic in São Paulo / illustrate a problem with urban planning

### 4.2 Qual é a função? (o professor lê cada trecho)
1. “Consider a city like Copenhagen, where 60% of people bike to work.”
2. “That said, cycling isn't practical in every climate.”
3. “The reason is simple: bike lanes are cheaper than highways.”
4. “So, cities that invest in bikes may reduce traffic in the long term.”
5. “Some people believe bikes are only for leisure.”
6. “A 2019 survey found that commuters who cycle report lower stress.”

### 4.3 Monte a sua mini palestra oral (2 min)
Tema: **Should schools start later in the morning?** Diga em sequência: afirmação → evidência/razão → exemplo → ressalva → conclusão. Use os sinais da seção 2.

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada (15 min)
🎧 O professor lê a palestra **uma vez**. Depois relê **apenas os 6 trechos numerados**; o aluno classifica cada um.
> Today let's talk about why some species become invasive. An invasive species is one that is introduced to a new area and spreads quickly, causing harm. **(1)** A common assumption is that invasive species are simply stronger than native ones. But that's not really the full picture. **(2)** Take the cane toad in Australia. It was introduced in 1935 to control beetles in sugar cane fields. Instead, it spread across millions of square kilometers. **(3)** The reason it succeeded wasn't strength — it was the absence of predators. Australian animals had never seen this toad, so they didn't know it was poisonous, and many died after eating it. **(4)** In fact, a study of northern quolls, a small native predator, found that their populations fell by more than 90% in some regions after the toads arrived. **(5)** Now, admittedly, not every introduced species becomes a problem. Most of them actually fail to survive. **(6)** So the lesson for scientists is that the new environment matters as much as the species itself.
**Classifique (A afirmação/crença · E exemplo · J justificativa · V evidência · R ressalva · C conclusão):**
1 __ 2 __ 3 __ 4 __ 5 __ 6 __
**Acertos:** __/6
**Perguntas orais:**
- Why does the professor mention the cane toad?
- Why does she mention the northern quoll study?
- What is the professor's main conclusion?
