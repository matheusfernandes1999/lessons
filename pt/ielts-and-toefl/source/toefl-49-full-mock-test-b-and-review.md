## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** simulado **B** cobrindo as 4 seções na **ordem da prova** (Reading → Listening → Writing → Speaking), em versão compacta, e análise por habilidade.
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** comparar seu desempenho com o simulado A e com a aula 1.
**Tarefa verificável:** executar simulado original e analisar resultado por habilidade.
**Observação:** versão compacta para a aula. Recomenda-se fazer um simulado completo oficial (ETS) fora da aula na mesma semana.

## 2. Reading (9 min)

> Bamboo is one of the fas___ (1) growing plants on Earth. Some spe___ (2) can grow almost one met___ (3) in a single day. Because it is stro___ (4) and light, bamboo is used in constr___ (5) in many Asian countries. It also abso___ (6) large amounts of carbon dioxide.
### R2. Daily Life (2 min)
> **Gym notice:** *From June 1, students must book fitness classes through the new app. Classes booked but not attended three times will result in a two-week booking suspension. The swimming pool schedule does not change.*
7. How will students book classes from June 1? 8. What happens after three missed bookings?
### R3. Academic Passage (4 min)
> (1) The Silk Road was not a single road but a network of trade routes connecting China with the Mediterranean for more than 1,500 years. Merchants rarely traveled the whole distance; instead, goods passed from trader to trader across many regions.
> (2) Although silk gave the network its name, many other products were exchanged, including spices, glass, paper and horses. Perhaps more importantly, ideas also traveled: religions, scientific knowledge and technologies such as papermaking spread along the routes.
> (3) The network declined after the fifteenth century, partly because sea routes became faster and safer for transporting goods.
9. Main idea of paragraph 1? 10. Why does the author say “Perhaps more importantly”? a) To show ideas were as or more significant than goods b) To say silk was unimportant c) To introduce the decline 11. Why did the network decline?

## 3. Listening (9 min)
🎧 Uma vez só.
### L1. Choose a Response
12. “Would you like to grab lunch after class?” — a) I'd love to. Where? b) I grabbed it. c) Lunch is at noon.
13. “I'm not sure I locked the lab door.” — a) Want me to go check? b) The lab is big. c) I'm sure.
### L2. Conversation
> **Student:** Hi, I'm trying to print my thesis, but my printing balance is zero.
> **Librarian:** You can add credit at the machine near the entrance — it takes cards. Or, since it's a thesis, you can use the graduate printing room on the third floor. It's free for graduate students.
> **Student:** I'm a graduate student! Do I need anything?
> **Librarian:** Just your ID. It's open until 8 tonight.
14. What is the student's problem? 15. What does the librarian suggest? 16. Until when is the room open?
### L3. Announcement
> Attention, residents of North Hall. The fire alarm test scheduled for Wednesday has been moved to Thursday at 10 a.m. The test will last about fifteen minutes. Residents do not need to leave the building.
17. When is the test now? 18. Do residents need to leave?
### L4. Academic Talk
> Let's look at why cities often develop near rivers. First, rivers provided fresh water for drinking and farming. Second, they were natural highways: before railways, moving goods by boat was much cheaper than by land. Take Paris and the Seine, for example — the river made trade possible from very early times. Today, rivers are less important for transport, but many cities still depend on them for water and, increasingly, for tourism and recreation.
19. Give two historical reasons cities developed near rivers. 20. Why does the speaker mention Paris?
