## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** **Matching Headings**: escolher, de uma lista, o título que resume **a ideia principal** de cada parágrafo. A armadilha: títulos que repetem **uma palavra** do parágrafo, mas não a ideia central.
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** resumir cada parágrafo em poucas palavras e escolher o título pela **função** (problema, causa, solução, exemplo, comparação…).
**Tarefa verificável:** associar títulos a seis parágrafos sem foco em palavras isoladas.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)
Dê um título curto (3–5 palavras) para:
- *your last weekend · your job or studies · your city's biggest problem*
O professor lê um parágrafo curto e o aluno cria um título:
> *Many teenagers today sleep less than eight hours a night. Schools start early, homework takes time, and phones keep them awake. As a result, many students feel tired in class.*
**Revisão da aula 20:** diga 3 marcadores de opinião do autor.

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
### Palavras de função em títulos (aprenda o que cada uma promete)
- **reasons for / causes of** — o parágrafo explica **por quê**
- **effects of / consequences of / impact of** — o parágrafo mostra **resultados**
- **a solution to / ways of dealing with** — **soluções**
- **a comparison between** — compara 2 coisas · **a contrast** — mostra diferença
- **an example of / a case study** — **exemplo específico**
- **the origins of / early history** — **começo** · **future prospects** — **futuro**
- **criticism of / doubts about** — **críticas** · **a misunderstanding / a common myth** — **ideia errada**
- **a surprising finding** — resultado inesperado · **practical applications** — usos práticos

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **consequences** = CON-se-quen-ces · **prospects** = PROS-pects · **misunderstanding** = mis-un-der-STAND-ing
- **cause / course · myth / mitt**
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> This paragraph isn't about the causes; it's about the consequences of the problem.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### Estratégia
1. **Leia os títulos** primeiro e note a **função** de cada um (causa? solução? exemplo?).
2. **Leia o parágrafo inteiro** rapidamente (não só a 1ª frase — às vezes a ideia principal está no meio ou no fim, depois de *however*).
3. **Resuma** em 3–5 palavras com suas palavras.
4. **Combine** o resumo com o título. Desconfie de títulos que só repetem uma palavra.
5. Comece pelos parágrafos **mais fáceis**; elimine títulos usados.
### Modelo
> **Paragraph:** *Plastic bags were introduced in the 1960s and quickly became popular because they were cheap and strong. However, it soon became clear that they caused serious environmental damage: they block drains, harm marine animals and take hundreds of years to break down.*
**Headings:** i) The popularity of plastic bags ii) The environmental cost of plastic bags iii) Alternatives to plastic
- Resumo: *bags popular → BUT big damage* → a ideia principal vem depois de **However** → **ii**
- i) é armadilha: fala de popularidade, mas é só o começo do parágrafo.

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: resumindo parágrafos
> This paragraph is mainly about **the causes of** **traffic jams**, not **the solutions**.
> → the effects / social media use / the history → an example / a successful recycling program / a general theory → criticism / the new law / its benefits
### 4.2 Resuma em 4 palavras
1. *In the 19th century, many children worked in factories for up to 14 hours a day. Laws to protect them were introduced slowly, and only after public campaigns revealed the terrible conditions.*
2. *Today, schools in Finland start formal reading lessons at the age of seven. Despite this late start, Finnish students consistently perform well in international reading tests.*
3. *Some scientists believe that the four-day week could reduce emissions, since fewer people would commute. However, others warn that people may simply travel more on their day off.*

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: 6 parágrafos (15 min)
**Instrução:** *Choose the correct heading for paragraphs A–F from the list below. There are more headings than paragraphs.*
> **List of Headings**
> i) Why the idea is spreading to other countries · ii) An unexpected benefit for older people · iii) How the scheme began · iv) A common criticism and a response · v) The economic cost of loneliness · vi) Training required for volunteers · vii) Measuring the results · viii) The problem the project aimed to solve · ix) A positive side effect for staff
> **The Loneliness Project**
> **A** Across many developed countries, the number of people living alone has risen sharply. Health experts now describe loneliness as a serious public health issue, linked to depression, heart disease and even shorter life expectancy.
> **B** In 2015, a small town in the Netherlands decided to act. A local supermarket opened a “chat checkout”, where customers who were in no hurry could stop and talk to the cashier. The idea came from a retired nurse who noticed that some elderly shoppers visited the store every day just to see a friendly face.
> **C** Within two years, researchers from a nearby university were asked to evaluate the scheme. They interviewed regular users and found that most felt more connected to their community. Several said the conversations were the only ones they had all day.
> **D** Interestingly, the researchers also discovered that the cashiers themselves reported higher job satisfaction. Many said the slower checkout made their work feel more meaningful, which was not one of the original goals.
> **E** Not everyone was enthusiastic. Some customers complained that the chat checkouts slowed down service. The company replied that they were optional and that other checkouts remained fast, and complaints soon decreased.
> **F** Today, similar initiatives have appeared in Belgium, the UK and Japan, partly because governments are looking for low-cost ways to reduce social isolation in ageing populations.
**Respostas:** A ___ B ___ C ___ D ___ E ___ F ___
**Acertos:** __/6
**Justifique (4 min):** para 3 respostas, explique por que o título escolhido é a **ideia principal**. Por que **ii**, **v** e **vi** são armadilhas?

## 7. Homework da semana
Estude **15–20 minutos por dia**.
**Dia 1 — Títulos.** Leia um artigo de 5 parágrafos e escreva um título de 4 palavras para cada.
**Dia 2 — Funções.** Classifique os parágrafos do dia 1 por função (causa, efeito, solução, exemplo, crítica, futuro).
**Dia 3 — Reading oficial.** Faça um Matching Headings oficial. Marque quais títulos eram armadilhas e por quê.
**Dia 4 — Crie.** Crie uma lista de 7 títulos (5 certos + 2 armadilhas) para o artigo do dia 1.
**Dia 5 — Speaking.** Grave 1 minuto resumindo o texto *The Loneliness Project*.
**Dia 6 — Vocabulário.** Faça frases com *initiative, scheme, evaluate, isolation, meaningful, optional*.
**Dia 7 — Revisão.** Faça o quiz abaixo.
