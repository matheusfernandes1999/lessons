## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** a tarefa mais temida: **True / False / Not Given** (sobre **fatos** do texto).
- **True:** o texto **confirma** (com outras palavras).
- **False:** o texto **diz o contrário**.
- **Not Given:** o texto **não diz** nada sobre isso (nem sim, nem não).
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** classificar afirmações e **apontar o trecho** que prova sua resposta.
**Tarefa verificável:** justificar oito classificações com trechos do texto.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)
O professor diz um fato sobre o aluno; outra pessoa “afirma” algo. True, False ou Not Given?
> **Fato:** *Ana lives in Recife and works as a nurse.*
> 1. Ana lives in Brazil. (T) · 2. Ana is a doctor. (F) · 3. Ana likes her job. (NG) · 4. Ana works in a hospital. (?)
Discuta a 4: *nurse* pode trabalhar em clínica, escola… → **NG**.
**Revisão da aula 18:** qual é a diferença entre skim e scan?

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
### Palavras que mudam tudo (leia com lupa!)
- **Quantidade:** *all / most / some / few / none* · *always / usually / sometimes / never*
- **Comparação:** *more than / less than / the first / the only / the largest*
- **Certeza:** *proved / showed* × *suggested / may / might / believed*
- **Tempo:** *before / after / since / until / originally / now*
### Paráfrases-chave
- *increase* = *rise / grow / go up* · *decrease* = *fall / decline / drop*
- *ancient* = *very old* · *nearly* = *almost* · *the majority* = *most*
### Expressões para justificar
- *The text says… which means… so it's True.* · *The text says the opposite: …* · *The text mentions X, but it doesn't say anything about Y, so it's Not Given.*

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **majority / minority** · **originally** = o-RIG-i-nal-ly · **contradict** = con-tra-DICT
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> The text mentions the price, but it doesn't say anything about the quality, so it's Not Given.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### Estratégia em 4 passos
1. Sublinhe a **âncora** (nome, número) e a **palavra-chave de sentido** (*first, only, most, increase*).
2. **Localize** pela âncora (as afirmações seguem a ordem do texto).
3. **Compare** palavra por palavra: o texto **confirma**, **contradiz** ou **não fala**?
4. **Não use conhecimento próprio!** Só o texto conta.
### Modelo
> **Texto:** *The Great Barrier Reef, located off the coast of Queensland, is the world's largest coral reef system. Since 1995, it has lost more than half of its coral, mainly due to rising sea temperatures.*
1. *The reef is near the coast of Queensland.* → **True** (*located off the coast of Queensland* = perto da costa)
2. *The reef is the largest in the world.* → **True** (*world's largest*)
3. *The reef has lost less than half of its coral since 1995.* → **False** (*more than half*)
4. *Tourism is the main cause of coral loss.* → **False** (*mainly due to rising sea temperatures*) — o texto dá outra causa principal.
5. *The reef attracts two million tourists a year.* → **Not Given**

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: justificando
> It's **False**, because the text says **“more than half”**, but the statement says **“less than half”**.
> → True / “the world's largest” / “the largest in the world” → Not Given / the text mentions temperature / it doesn't mention tourists
### 4.2 Classifique e justifique
> **Text:** *Honey never spoils if it is stored correctly. Archaeologists have found pots of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs that were around 3,000 years old and still edible. This is because honey contains very little water and is naturally acidic, which prevents bacteria from growing. However, if honey absorbs moisture from the air, it can ferment.*
1. Honey can last for thousands of years.
2. All the honey found in Egyptian tombs was edible.
3. Honey contains a lot of water.
4. Honey can ferment if it absorbs moisture.
5. Egyptians used honey as medicine.

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: 8 afirmações (15 min)
### Leia (3 min) e classifique (7 min)
> **The Rise of Electric Scooters**
> Shared electric scooters first appeared in Santa Monica, California, in 2017, and within two years they were available in more than 100 cities worldwide. Users unlock the scooters with a smartphone app and pay by the minute.
> Supporters claim that e-scooters reduce car journeys, but research so far is mixed. A study in Paris found that most scooter trips replaced walking or public transport rather than car trips. In addition, the scooters themselves often last only a few months, which raises questions about their environmental benefits.
> Safety is another concern. Hospitals in several cities have reported an increase in head injuries, and some cities, including Madrid, have temporarily banned the scooters. Others have introduced speed limits and designated parking areas.
> Despite these problems, operators have improved the design of their vehicles, and newer models are said to last considerably longer.
1. Shared e-scooters were first introduced in California.
2. By 2019, e-scooters were available in over 100 cities.
3. Users pay a fixed price per trip.
4. The Paris study showed that most scooter trips replaced car trips.
5. Early scooters had a short lifespan.
6. Madrid permanently banned e-scooters.
7. Some cities have created special parking areas for scooters.
8. Newer scooters are cheaper to rent.
**Acertos:** __/8
### Justifique (5 min)
Para cada resposta, leia em voz alta o trecho que prova (ou diga “the text doesn't mention…”).
