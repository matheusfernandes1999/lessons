## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** a **Part 4**: uma palestra acadêmica **sem pausa no meio**, geralmente com **10 lacunas** em notas. É a parte mais longa e mais densa.
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** seguir a estrutura da palestra pelos sinais de transição e completar notas sem se perder.
**Tarefa verificável:** completar notas de uma palestra acadêmica.

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
### Sinais de estrutura (signposting)
- **Início:** *Today I'm going to talk about… · I'll begin by… · I'd like to look at three aspects…*
- **Mudança:** *Moving on to… · Let's now turn to… · Another important factor is…*
- **Exemplo:** *For instance… · A good example of this is… · Take… for example.*
- **Ênfase:** *What's particularly interesting is… · The key point here is…*
- **Conclusão:** *To sum up… · So, in conclusion…*
### Vocabulário acadêmico frequente na Part 4
- **material · structure · process · technique · species · climate · settlement · invention · decline · survival · evidence · ancient · modern · traditional · durable · flexible**

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **ancient** = AIN-shent · **durable** = DYOO-ra-ble · **settlement** = SET-tl-ment
- **mould / mold · fibre / fiber** (grafias britânica/americana)
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> Moving on to the second aspect, what's particularly interesting is how ancient builders made the material more durable.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### Estratégia para a Part 4
1. **Use o tempo de leitura** para ler **todas** as notas e entender a estrutura (títulos e subtítulos = mapa da palestra).
2. **Os títulos das notas = sinais da palestra.** Quando ouvir *Moving on to…*, olhe para o próximo título.
3. **Não pare** se perder uma lacuna: pule e siga para a próxima — o áudio não espera.
4. **Gramática da nota:** a lacuna costuma ser substantivo; confira plural.
### Modelo
> **Notas:** *Roman concrete — main ingredient: volcanic (1) ___ · Advantage: became stronger in contact with (2) ___*
🎧 > “Roman concrete was made using lime and volcanic ash. What's particularly interesting is that it actually got stronger over time when it came into contact with sea water — which is why Roman harbours are still standing.”
→ (1) **ash** (2) **sea water** (ou *seawater*)

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: Part 4 (15 min)
**Instrução:** *Complete the notes. Write ONE WORD ONLY for each answer.* (1 min para ler antes)
> **The History of Glass**
> **Origins**
> - First glass objects: small (1) ______ made in Mesopotamia about 4,500 years ago
> - Probably discovered by accident when sand was heated near a (2) ______
> **Roman period**
> - Invention of glassblowing → glass became cheaper and more (3) ______
> - Romans used glass for windows, though it was not very (4) ______
> **Medieval Europe**
> - Venice: glassmakers moved to the island of Murano because of the risk of (5) ______
> - Workers were not allowed to (6) ______ the island
> **Modern glass**
> - 1950s: “float” process → perfectly flat glass for (7) ______ and cars
> - Today: research into glass that can (8) ______ itself
> - Glass can be recycled without losing (9) ______
> - Main challenge: collecting and (10) ______ different colours
🎧 Palestra (professor lê sem pausas, ≈3 min):
> “Good morning. Today we'll trace the history of glass, from ancient times to the present. Let's begin with its origins. The earliest glass objects we know of are small beads, made in Mesopotamia roughly four and a half thousand years ago. Nobody knows exactly how glass was discovered, but one theory is that it happened by accident, when sand was heated near a fire used for cooking or metalwork.
> Moving on to the Roman period. The great breakthrough was the invention of glassblowing, which allowed glass to be shaped quickly. As a result, glass became cheaper and more common — ordinary families could own glass cups. The Romans even used glass in windows, although it wasn't very clear; you could see light, but not much detail.
> Now let's turn to medieval Europe, and in particular Venice. In 1291, the city ordered its glassmakers to move to the island of Murano. The official reason was the risk of fire in a city of wooden buildings — though it also helped keep their techniques secret. In fact, glassmakers were not permitted to leave the island without permission.
> Finally, modern glass. In the 1950s, a British company developed the float process, in which molten glass is poured onto a bed of liquid tin. This produced perfectly flat glass, ideal for buildings and cars. Today, researchers are working on glass that can repair itself when it's scratched. And what's particularly interesting from an environmental point of view is that glass can be recycled again and again without losing quality. The main challenge is practical: collecting it and separating the different colours, which can't be mixed.
> To sum up, a material discovered by accident has become one of the most important in modern life.”
**Acertos:** __/10
**Fala (3 min):** com base nas notas, explique em 1 minuto as 4 fases da história do vidro.
