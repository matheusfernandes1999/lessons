## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** no IELTS Reading você **não tem tempo** de ler tudo devagar. Duas técnicas: **skimming** (ler rápido para entender a estrutura) e **scanning** (procurar uma informação específica).
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** mapear um texto em 2 minutos e localizar respostas sem reler tudo.
**Tarefa verificável:** mapear dois textos e localizar oito respostas.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)
- How do you read the news: every word, or just headlines and first lines?
- How do you find a phone number on a long web page?

> *The first public library in the modern sense opened in Manchester in 1852, funded by local taxes and free for all residents.*

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
- **skim** — passar os olhos (ideia geral) · **scan** — rastrear (dado específico)
- **topic sentence** — frase-tópico (geralmente a 1ª do parágrafo)
- **keyword** — palavra-chave · **locate** — localizar · **paragraph map** — mapa de parágrafos
- **proper noun** — nome próprio · **date / figure / percentage**
Palavras-guia fáceis de achar (*scan anchors*): **nomes próprios, números, datas, palavras em itálico/aspas, termos técnicos**.
Palavras acadêmicas comuns em textos IELTS:
- **significant · consequently · approximately · decline · emerge · originate · phenomenon · approach · factor · impact**

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **phenomenon** = phe-NOM-e-non · **approximately** = ap-PROX-i-mate-ly · **emerge** = e-MERGE · **originate** = o-RIG-i-nate
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> I skimmed the passage first and then scanned for dates and names.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### Skimming em 2 minutos
1. Leia o **título** e o **subtítulo**.
2. Leia a **1ª frase de cada parágrafo** (e às vezes a última).
3. Escreva um **mapa**: 2–4 palavras por parágrafo.
### Scanning
1. Escolha a **âncora** da pergunta (nome, número, termo).
2. Mova os olhos **rápido** procurando só essa forma.
3. Achou? Leia **a frase inteira** + 1 antes/depois para responder.
### Modelo
> **A** The invention of the bicycle in the 19th century transformed personal transport… **B** At first, bicycles were expensive and dangerous… **C** The “safety bicycle” of the 1880s, with two equal wheels, changed everything… **D** Today, bicycles are promoted as a solution to urban traffic…
**Mapa:** A invenção/impacto · B problemas iniciais · C safety bicycle 1880s · D hoje/cidades
**Pergunta:** *When did bicycles with two equal wheels appear?* → âncora *equal wheels* → parágrafo C → **1880s**

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: descrevendo o mapa
> Paragraph **A** introduces **the topic**, paragraph **B** describes **early problems**, and paragraph **C** explains **a key invention**.
> → gives examples / compares two theories / presents a solution / discusses the future
### 4.2 Skim (2 min) — faça o mapa
> **Coffee's Journey**
> **A** Coffee is now one of the most traded agricultural products in the world, yet its origins are surprisingly uncertain.
> **B** According to a popular legend, an Ethiopian goat herder named Kaldi noticed his goats became energetic after eating red berries. Historians, however, have found no written evidence of this story before the 17th century.
> **C** What is certain is that by the 15th century, coffee was being cultivated in Yemen, where Sufi monks drank it to stay awake during night prayers.
> **D** From Yemen, coffee spread to Mecca, Cairo and Istanbul. Coffee houses emerged as centres of conversation, and some rulers tried to ban them, fearing political discussion.
> **E** Europeans encountered coffee in the 1600s. Within decades, cafés opened in Venice, London and Paris, and colonial powers began planting coffee in Java, the Caribbean and, later, Brazil.
### 4.3 Scan (30 s por pergunta)
1. Name of the goat herder? 2. Century coffee was grown in Yemen? 3. Why did some rulers try to ban coffee houses? 4. Name two European cities with early cafés.

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: 2 textos, 8 respostas (15 min)
### Texto 1 — skim 1 min + 4 perguntas em 4 min
> **Vertical Forests**
> **A** In 2014, two residential towers in Milan, known as Bosco Verticale, were completed. Together they hold around 900 trees and more than 20,000 smaller plants.
> **B** The architect, Stefano Boeri, designed the buildings to increase biodiversity in the city and to absorb pollution. The plants also provide shade, reducing the need for air conditioning in summer.
> **C** Maintenance is a challenge. Gardeners, sometimes called “flying gardeners”, are lowered from the roof on ropes to prune the trees three times a year.
> **D** Similar projects have since been proposed in China, the Netherlands and Egypt, although critics argue that the cost of construction and maintenance makes them affordable only for wealthy residents.
1. In what year were the towers completed? 2. Approximately how many trees do they contain? 3. How often are the trees pruned? 4. What is the main criticism?
### Texto 2 — skim 1 min + 4 perguntas em 4 min
> **The Science of Yawning**
> **A** Almost all vertebrates yawn, including fish and birds, which suggests the behaviour is very ancient.
> **B** For many years, it was believed that we yawn to take in more oxygen. Experiments in the 1980s, however, found that breathing extra oxygen did not reduce yawning.
> **C** A more recent theory, proposed by psychologist Andrew Gallup, is that yawning helps to cool the brain. People yawn more often in mild temperatures than in very hot or very cold conditions.
> **D** Yawning is also contagious. Studies show that people are more likely to “catch” a yawn from family members than from strangers, which may be linked to empathy.
5. Which animals besides mammals yawn? 6. When was the oxygen theory tested? 7. Who proposed the brain-cooling theory? 8. Who are we most likely to catch a yawn from?
