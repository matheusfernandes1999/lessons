### A3. Academic Passage (8 min)
> (1) Sleep is not unique to humans; almost all animals sleep in some form. However, the way they sleep varies widely. Dolphins, for example, practice "unihemispheric sleep": one half of the brain sleeps while the other stays awake. This allows them to keep swimming and come to the surface to breathe.
> (2) Some birds use the same strategy during long migrations. Frigatebirds, which can fly for weeks over the ocean without landing, have been recorded sleeping for only about 40 minutes a day while flying, often with one eye open.
> (3) Scientists believe these adaptations show how essential sleep is: rather than eliminating sleep, evolution found ways to fit it into dangerous or demanding lifestyles. Still, why sleep is so important remains one of biology's open questions. Leading hypotheses include memory consolidation, energy conservation and the removal of waste products from the brain.

Why does the author mention frigatebirds? a) As another example of sleeping with half the brain b) To describe migration routes c) To show that birds don't sleep 14. In paragraph 3, “eliminating” is closest to: a) removing b) studying c) increasing 15. ## 4. Bloco B — Listening (15 min)
🎧 **Professor:** leia tudo uma vez, sem pausas extras.
### B1. Choose a Response
16. “Do you mind if I borrow your stapler?” — a) Not at all, go ahead. b) Yes, it's a stapler. c) I borrowed it.
17. “I heard the library's closing early today.” — a) Really? Do you know why? b) I'm closing the book. c) It's early.
18. “Shouldn't we start the presentation with the data?” — a) Good point, let's do that. b) I started yesterday. c) The data is big.
### B2. Conversation
> **Student:** Hi, I'd like to change my meal plan. I have the 20-meals-a-week plan, but I never eat breakfast in the dining hall.
> **Officer:** OK. We have a 14-meal plan, which is cheaper. You can switch until the end of next week.
> **Student:** Will I get money back?
> **Officer:** Not money, but the difference goes into your dining credit, which you can use at the campus café.
> **Student:** Perfect. Can I do it online?
> **Officer:** Yes, through the student portal, under “Housing and Dining.”
19. What does the student want? 20. What happens to the price difference? 21. How can the student make the change?
### B3. Announcement
> Attention, all biology majors. The Biology Department's research poster session, originally planned for the Science Center lobby, will now be held in the Student Union Ballroom because of the expected number of visitors. The event is on Friday from 2 to 5 p.m. Students presenting posters must arrive by 1:15 to set up. Faculty from other departments have been invited.
22. Why was the location changed? 23. What time must presenters arrive?
### B4. Academic Talk
> Today let's talk about why some languages disappear. Linguists estimate that about 7,000 languages are spoken today, and many of them could disappear by the end of this century. The main reason isn't that people forget them — it's that parents stop passing them to their children, usually because another language offers better economic opportunities. Take Welsh, for example. In the twentieth century, the number of speakers fell sharply. But thanks to bilingual schools and Welsh-language media, the decline slowed, and today more young people speak it. So, language loss can be reversed, but only with strong community and government support.
24. What is the main reason languages disappear? 25. Why does the professor mention Welsh?
