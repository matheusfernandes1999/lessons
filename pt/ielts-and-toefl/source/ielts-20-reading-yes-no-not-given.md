## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** **Yes / No / Not Given** é parecido com TFNG, mas sobre **opiniões e afirmações do autor** (não fatos).
- **Yes:** a afirmação concorda com a **visão do autor**.
- **No:** contradiz a visão do autor.
- **Not Given:** o autor não expressa opinião sobre isso.
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** distinguir **o que o autor pensa** do que **outras pessoas pensam** no texto.
**Tarefa verificável:** classificar oito afirmações sobre uma argumentação.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)
O professor lê uma opinião; o aluno diz se **o professor** concorda (Yes), discorda (No) ou não disse (NG):
> *“Some people say homework is useless. I disagree: short, regular homework helps students remember. However, I think teachers give too much homework at weekends.”*
1. Homework is useless. → ___ 2. Short homework is useful. → ___ 3. Weekend homework should be reduced. → ___ 4. Homework should be graded. → ___
**Revisão da aula 19:** explique a diferença entre False e Not Given.

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
### Como o autor mostra opinião
- **Verbos:** *argue, believe, claim, maintain, suggest, doubt, question, reject*
- **Adjetivos avaliativos:** *convincing, flawed, misleading, essential, overrated, unfortunate, remarkable*
- **Advérbios:** *clearly, surely, arguably, unfortunately, admittedly, rightly, wrongly*
- **Modais:** *should, must, ought to, need to*
### Opinião dos outros × do autor
- *Critics argue that… · Some people believe… · It is often said that…* → **não é** necessariamente o autor!
- *However, I… · In my view… · This is misleading because… · Rightly/wrongly…* → **é** o autor.

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **arguably** = AR-gu-a-bly · **misleading** = mis-LEAD-ing · **admittedly** = ad-MIT-ted-ly
- **doubt** (b mudo: /daʊt/) · **flawed / floored** (iguais em inglês britânico)
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> Critics argue that the plan is too expensive, but this view is arguably misleading.

:::quiz vocab
? mc|vocabulary|“Critics argue that X. However, this view is misleading.” The author thinks X is…
A) correct || Não.
*B) wrong || Correto: misleading = enganoso.
C) not mentioned || O autor opina.
? mc|vocabulary|“The government rightly decided to…” The author thinks the decision was…
*A) correct || Correto.
B) wrong || Seria “wrongly”.
? w|vocabulary|The word “doubt” has a silent letter: ______
> b
= doubt — o “b” é mudo.
? mc|comprehension|Yes/No/Not Given is about…
A) facts || Isso é TFNG.
*B) the writer's views or claims || Correto.
? w|vocabulary|“Some people believe…” shows the opinion of ______, not necessarily the author. (outros: other ___)
> people
= other people
:::

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### Estratégia
1. **Separe as vozes:** sublinhe frases do autor (*I, In my view, however, clearly, should*) e circule as de outros (*critics, some people*).
2. Localize o tema da afirmação.
3. Pergunte: **o autor** concorda, discorda ou não opina?
### Modelo
> *Many economists claim that tourism always benefits local communities. This is an oversimplification. While tourism creates jobs, much of the profit often goes to international hotel chains. Governments should therefore require foreign companies to employ local staff. Whether tourism should be limited in historic cities is a separate question.*
1. *Tourism always benefits local communities.* → **No** (*This is an oversimplification*)
2. *Tourism creates jobs.* → **Yes** (*While tourism creates jobs*)
3. *Foreign companies should hire local workers.* → **Yes** (*should require… to employ local staff*)
4. *Tourism should be limited in historic cities.* → **Not Given** (*a separate question* — sem opinião)

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: identificando a voz
> **Critics** say that **e-books will replace paper books**, but **the author** believes that **both will continue to exist**.
> → Some parents / video games are harmful / they can develop problem-solving → Many politicians / cars are essential / cities can work without them
### 4.2 Yes, No ou NG?
> *It is often claimed that learning a second language in childhood is easier than in adulthood. The evidence for this is weaker than many assume. Adults, in fact, often learn grammar faster than children because they can use analytical strategies. Children do, however, tend to achieve better pronunciation. Schools, in my opinion, should start language teaching early, not because children learn faster, but because they have more years to practise.*
1. Children always learn languages faster than adults.
2. Adults may learn grammar more quickly than children.
3. Children usually develop better pronunciation.
4. Schools should start language teaching early.
5. Language classes should be compulsory.

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: 8 afirmações (15 min)
### Leia (4 min) e classifique (6 min)
> **Should We Work Less?**
> In recent years, several companies have experimented with a four-day working week. Supporters claim that shorter weeks make employees happier and more productive. The results of the trials so far are encouraging, but we should be careful not to draw conclusions too quickly.
> First, most of the companies that took part chose to do so, and were therefore probably well suited to the change. It would be wrong to assume that every industry could adopt the same model. Hospitals and schools, for example, cannot simply close one day a week.
> Second, the idea that productivity always rises when hours fall is, frankly, unproven. In some trials, employees reported being more stressed because they had to complete the same work in less time.
> That said, I am convinced that the traditional forty-hour week is outdated for many office jobs. Much of the time spent in offices is not productive, and technology allows tasks to be completed faster than in the past. Rather than a single national policy, governments should encourage companies to experiment with flexible schedules. Whether this would also improve gender equality at work is a fascinating question, but one that requires its own research.
1. The results of four-day week trials have been positive so far.
2. The trials prove that all industries can adopt a four-day week.
3. Hospitals could easily close one day a week.
4. Productivity always increases when working hours are reduced.
5. Some employees felt more stressed during the trials.
6. The forty-hour week is no longer suitable for many office jobs.
7. Governments should introduce a national four-day week law.
8. A four-day week would improve gender equality.
**Acertos:** __/8
### Justifique (5 min)
Leia o trecho que prova cada resposta.

## 7. Homework da semana
Estude **15–20 minutos por dia**.
**Dia 1 — Marcadores.** Faça uma lista de 20 palavras que mostram opinião (verbos, adjetivos, advérbios).
**Dia 2 — Artigo de opinião.** Leia um *opinion piece* (ex.: seção Opinion de um jornal). Sublinhe as frases do autor e circule as de outros.
**Dia 3 — Crie YNNG.** Crie 6 afirmações (2 Y, 2 N, 2 NG) sobre o artigo do dia 2.
**Dia 4 — Reading oficial.** Faça um exercício YNNG oficial e justifique.
**Dia 5 — Speaking.** Grave 1 minuto dando sua opinião sobre a semana de 4 dias e citando a opinião de “critics”.
**Dia 6 — Writing.** Escreva 100 palavras usando *Critics argue that… However, in my view…*
**Dia 7 — Revisão.** Faça o quiz abaixo.
