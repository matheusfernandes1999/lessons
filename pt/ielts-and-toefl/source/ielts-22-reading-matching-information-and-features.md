## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** dois tipos de matching:
- **Matching Information:** *Which paragraph contains…?* — encontrar **onde** está uma informação (as perguntas **não** seguem a ordem do texto!).
- **Matching Features:** ligar **afirmações a pessoas/datas/lugares** (*Which researcher said…?*).
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** localizar informações espalhadas e ligar ideias às pessoas certas, com evidência.
**Tarefa verificável:** resolver oito correspondências com evidência.

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
### Expressões típicas das perguntas de Matching Information
- **a reference to** — menção a · **a description of** — descrição de · **an explanation of** — explicação de
- **an example of** · **a comparison between** · **a prediction about** — previsão
- **a suggestion that** — sugestão · **a reason why** · **a method for** · **a warning about**
### Pistas de “tipo de informação”
- **prediction** → *will, by 2050, is expected to, in the future*
- **comparison** → *than, whereas, similarly, unlike*
- **warning** → *risk, danger, could lead to, should be careful*
- **method** → *by + -ing, technique, approach, using*
### Verbos de pesquisadores (Matching Features)
- **found · showed · claimed · argued · suggested · questioned · warned · proposed**

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **reference** = REF-er-ence · **prediction** = pre-DIC-tion · **comparison** = com-PAR-i-son
- **warn / worn / one** · **claim / climb**
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> Paragraph C contains a prediction, while paragraph E makes a comparison.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### Matching Information — estratégia
1. Leia **todas as perguntas** e identifique o **tipo** de informação (exemplo, previsão, custo…).
2. Leia o texto **parágrafo por parágrafo**; ao terminar cada um, olhe a lista: *este parágrafo contém algum desses itens?*
3. Um parágrafo pode ser usado **mais de uma vez** (leia a instrução: *You may use any letter more than once*).
### Matching Features — estratégia
1. **Scan** os nomes no texto e sublinhe todos (podem aparecer mais de uma vez!).
2. Leia o que cada pessoa disse/encontrou.
3. Combine com as afirmações (paráfrase!).
### Modelo
> **A** … Dr. Kim **found** that children who read daily had larger vocabularies. **B** … By 2030, digital textbooks **are expected to** replace most printed ones. **C** … Professor Ruiz, however, **warned** that screens may reduce concentration…
- *A prediction about education materials* → **B**
- *Who warned about a negative effect of screens?* → **Ruiz** (C)
- *Who linked reading habits to vocabulary size?* → **Kim** (A)

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada (15 min)
> **Cities and Heat**
> **A** Urban areas can be up to 7°C warmer than the surrounding countryside, a phenomenon known as the urban heat island. Dark surfaces such as asphalt absorb sunlight during the day and release heat at night.
> **B** Dr. Helen Carter, a climate scientist, measured temperatures across London using sensors fixed to buses. Her results showed that the hottest areas were those with the fewest trees, rather than those with the most traffic.
> **C** Some cities have responded by painting roofs white. In one experiment in New York, white roofs were found to be up to 24°C cooler than black roofs on summer afternoons.
> **D** However, engineer Marco Bellini argues that white roofs alone are not enough. He believes that cities should prioritise “green corridors” — lines of trees and parks — that allow cooler air to flow through streets.
> **E** Not every solution has been successful. In one Spanish city, artificial grass was installed in public squares, but it actually became hotter than the asphalt it replaced and was later removed.
> **F** Experts predict that, by 2050, more than two-thirds of the world's population will live in cities, making heat one of the most important urban health issues of the century.
**Matching Information** — *Which paragraph contains…?*
1. a prediction about population growth ___ 2. an example of a solution that failed ___ 3. a description of how temperatures were measured ___ 4. an explanation of why cities are warmer ___
**Matching Features** — *Who/What: A Helen Carter · B Marco Bellini · C the New York experiment*
5. found that trees mattered more than traffic ___ 6. showed a large temperature difference between roof colours ___ 7. recommended allowing air to move through the city ___ 8. suggested that one solution is insufficient on its own ___
**Acertos:** __/8 · **Evidência oral (3 min):** leia o trecho de 4 respostas.
