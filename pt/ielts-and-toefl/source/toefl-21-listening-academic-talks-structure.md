## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** *Listen to an Academic Talk*: entender a **estrutura** de uma palestra — tópico, subtemas e as **expressões de transição** que o professor usa para mudar de parte.
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** ouvir uma palestra curta e desenhar um mapa com o tópico e 2–3 subtemas.
**Tarefa verificável:** esboçar um mapa de ideias de uma palestra curta.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)
- Do you like watching lectures or talks online? (TED talks, YouTube classes…)

- Explain how to make your favorite dish in 3 steps. Use *first, then, finally*.

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
### Sinais de estrutura (signposting) — ouça e reconheça
- **Introduzir o tópico:** *Today we're going to look at… / Today's topic is…*
- **Listar partes:** *There are three main reasons… / First,… Second,… Finally,…*
- **Mudar de assunto:** *Now let's turn to… / Moving on to… / Let's look at…*
- **Voltar:** *Going back to… / As I mentioned earlier,…*
- **Exemplificar:** *For example,… / Take… for instance.*
- **Destacar:** *The key point is… / What's important here is…*
- **Definir:** *By X, I mean… / X refers to…*
- **Concluir:** *So, to sum up,… / In short,…*
- **Digressão:** *By the way,… / Just as a side note,…* (geralmente não cai na prova!)
### Vocabulário acadêmico
- **migration** — migração · **adaptation** — adaptação · **survive** — sobreviver
- **predator / prey** — predador / presa · **habitat** — habitat

### Pronúncia: palavras de transição
Ouça e repita (ritmo natural):
- *Now let's TURN to…* · *MOVing ON to…* · *to SUM UP…* · *as I MENtioned EARlier…*
Ouça, compare e repita: **turn / torn / tune** · **sum / some** (iguais!) · **prey / pray** (iguais!)
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> Now let's turn to the second reason: predators and prey.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### O mapa de ideias
Não escreva frases. Desenhe:
> TÓPICO (no centro/topo)
> ├── Subtema 1 → exemplo/detalhe
> ├── Subtema 2 → exemplo/detalhe
> └── Subtema 3 → exemplo/detalhe
> Conclusão: …
### Modelo
🎧 > Today we're going to look at why some birds migrate thousands of kilometers every year. There are basically two main reasons. First, food. In winter, insects and plants become scarce in the north, so birds fly south where food is available. Take the Arctic tern, for instance — it travels from the Arctic to Antarctica. Now, let's turn to the second reason: breeding. Many birds return north in spring because the long summer days give them more time to feed their chicks. By the way, I once saw thousands of geese flying over my house — amazing. Anyway, to sum up, migration is a trade-off: the journey is dangerous, but the benefits of food and breeding are greater.
**Mapa:**
> Bird migration — why?
> ├── 1. Food → winter: fewer insects in the north → fly south (ex.: Arctic tern)
> ├── 2. Breeding → long summer days → more time to feed chicks
> └── Conclusion: dangerous trip, but benefits > risks
> (geese story = digression ✘)

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: reconstruindo a estrutura
> The professor **introduces** the topic of **bird migration** and then **explains two reasons**.
> → defines / photosynthesis / gives two examples → compares / two theories / criticizes one → describes / a process / lists four stages

### 4.2 Identifique a função
🎧 O professor lê cada frase; o aluno diz a função (introdução, lista, mudança, exemplo, definição, conclusão, digressão).
1. “By 'urban sprawl', I mean the uncontrolled growth of cities.”
2. “Moving on to the economic effects…”
3. “Just as a side note, my grandfather was a farmer.”
4. “Take Tokyo, for example.”
5. “So, in short, cities need better planning.”
6. “Today's lecture is about urban sprawl.”

### 4.3 Mini palestra 2 (mapa rápido)
🎧 > Today I want to talk about how plants defend themselves. Plants can't run away, so they've developed other strategies. The first is physical defense: think of thorns on roses or the hard bark of trees. The second strategy is chemical. Some plants produce toxins that make them taste bad or even poisonous — tobacco, for example, produces nicotine, which is toxic to many insects. Finally, and this is the most surprising, some plants call for help. When caterpillars attack corn, the plant releases chemicals that attract wasps, and the wasps attack the caterpillars. So, plants are far from passive.

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: mapa de uma palestra (15 min)
🎧 O professor lê **uma vez** (cerca de 2 min). O aluno desenha o mapa enquanto ouve.
> Good morning. Today we're going to talk about the history of coffee and how it spread around the world. Let's start with its origins. According to legend, coffee was discovered in Ethiopia, where a goat herder noticed that his goats became very energetic after eating certain berries. Whether that story is true or not, we know that by the fifteenth century, coffee was being grown in Yemen.
> Moving on to the second stage: the spread to the Middle East and Europe. Coffee houses became popular in cities like Istanbul and Cairo, where people met to talk, play games and discuss news. In the seventeenth century, coffee reached Europe. At first, some people were suspicious of it — they called it "the bitter invention of Satan" — but soon coffee houses opened in London, Paris and Vienna. In London, some coffee houses were called "penny universities," because for the price of a cup, you could listen to intellectual conversations. By the way, my favorite coffee is from Colombia, but that's a different story.
> Now, the third stage: coffee in the Americas. In the eighteenth century, European powers brought coffee plants to their colonies. Brazil, in particular, became the world's largest producer in the nineteenth century, a position it still holds today. Unfortunately, this expansion depended heavily on enslaved labor.
> So, to sum up: coffee went from Ethiopia to Yemen, then to the Middle East and Europe, and finally to the Americas, changing social life and economies along the way.
**Meu mapa:**
> Tópico: ______
> ├── 1. ______ → ______
> ├── 2. ______ → ______
> ├── 3. ______ → ______
> └── Conclusão: ______
**Relato oral (3 min):** usando o mapa, explique a palestra em 1 minuto com *first, then, moving on, to sum up*.
**Perguntas (professor faz oralmente):** 1. What is the legend about? 2. Why were some coffee houses called “penny universities”? 3. Which part was a digression? 4. What was negative about coffee's expansion in the Americas?
