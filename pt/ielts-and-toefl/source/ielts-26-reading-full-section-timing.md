## 3. Simulado compacto (28 min)
### Passage 1 — meta 7 min (mais fácil)
> **The Humble Pencil**
> The modern pencil dates back to the 1560s, when a large deposit of pure graphite was discovered in Borrowdale, in northern England. Local people first used it to mark sheep. Because graphite is soft and breaks easily, it was soon wrapped in string, and later inserted into wooden holders.
> For centuries, English graphite was so valuable that the mines were guarded, and smuggling was a serious crime. In 1795, during a war that cut France off from English supplies, the French engineer Nicolas-Jacques Conté developed a method of mixing powdered graphite with clay. This not only solved the supply problem but also allowed manufacturers to control hardness: more clay produced a harder pencil.
> Today, around 14 billion pencils are produced every year. Despite digital technology, they remain popular because they are cheap, work in extreme temperatures, and do not need electricity.
1. Graphite was first used in Borrowdale to mark animals. (T/F/NG)
2. The Borrowdale mines were protected because graphite was valuable. (T/F/NG)
3. Conté was English. (T/F/NG)
4. Adding more ______ made pencils harder. (ONE WORD)
5. Pencils are popular partly because they don't need ______. (ONE WORD)
### Passage 2 — meta 9 min (média)
> **A** Many people believe that goldfish have a memory span of only three seconds. This idea is widely repeated but has no scientific basis.
> **B** In experiments at the University of Plymouth, goldfish were trained to push a lever to receive food. When the lever worked only at a certain time of day, the fish learned to push it at that time.
> **C** In another study, fish learned to associate a particular sound with feeding and responded to that sound months later, suggesting long-term memory.
> **D** Researchers argue that the myth may have survived because it makes people feel less guilty about keeping fish in small bowls. Some animal welfare groups now campaign against such bowls, recommending larger tanks with plants and filters.
**Headings (choose for A–D):** i) Evidence of learning linked to time ii) A popular but false belief iii) Why the myth may persist iv) Memory lasting a long time v) How goldfish are bred
6. A ___ 7. B ___ 8. C ___ 9. D ___
### Passage 3 — meta 12 min (mais difícil)
> **Does Multitasking Work?**
> Many people claim they can work effectively while checking messages, listening to podcasts, or switching between tasks. Research, however, suggests that what we call multitasking is usually rapid switching, and each switch has a cost.
> In a well-known study at Stanford University, participants who described themselves as heavy multitaskers performed worse on tests of attention than those who rarely multitasked. Surprisingly, they were also worse at switching between tasks — the very skill they believed they had developed.
> Not all researchers interpret these findings in the same way. Some suggest that people who multitask a lot may already have weaker attention control, rather than multitasking causing the problem. Distinguishing cause from correlation, they argue, requires long-term studies that have not yet been carried out.
> Nevertheless, there is broad agreement that some combinations are less harmful than others. Activities that use different resources — such as walking while listening to music — interfere relatively little. Tasks that require the same resource, like reading an email while listening to a lecture, compete directly for attention.
> The practical lesson, the writer believes, is not to abandon multitasking entirely but to be selective: protect focused time for demanding work, and save routine tasks for moments when attention is less critical.
10. *What did the Stanford study find?* A) heavy multitaskers were better at switching B) heavy multitaskers performed worse on attention tests C) multitasking improves memory
11. *What do some researchers question?* A) whether multitasking causes weaker attention B) whether the Stanford study existed C) whether music helps concentration
12. *Which combination interferes least?* A) reading and listening to a lecture B) walking and listening to music C) two emails at once
13. *What is the writer's view?* (YES/NO/NOT GIVEN) — *People should completely stop multitasking.* ___
14. *Long-term studies on this question have already been completed.* (TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN) ___
