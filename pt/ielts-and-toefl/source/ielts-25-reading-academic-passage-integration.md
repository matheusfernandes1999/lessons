## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
### Palavras de ligação entre parágrafos (cohesion)
- **referência para trás:** *this, these, such, the former/latter, the above, as mentioned*
- **contraste entre partes:** *however, by contrast, on the other hand, nevertheless*
- **adição:** *furthermore, in addition, similarly*
- **resultado:** *consequently, as a result, therefore*
### Vocabulário do texto de hoje (urbanismo)
- **density** — densidade · **sprawl** — expansão desordenada · **commute** — trajeto
- **infrastructure** · **zoning** — zoneamento · **amenities** — serviços/comodidades
- **pedestrian** — pedestre · **mixed-use** — uso misto (moradia + comércio)

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **infrastructure** = IN-fra-struc-ture · **pedestrian** = pe-DES-tri-an · **amenities** = a-MEN-i-ties
**Frase para repetir 3 vezes:**
> Mixed-use neighbourhoods give pedestrians easy access to amenities and reduce commuting.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### Ordem de ataque em um texto misto
1. **Skim** (2 min) e faça o mapa de parágrafos.
2. Comece pelas tarefas que **seguem a ordem** (TFNG, completar frases/resumo) — elas “andam” pelo texto.
3. Depois, **Matching Information/Headings** (já com o mapa pronto).
4. Por último, perguntas **globais** (título do texto, propósito geral).
### Perguntas que cruzam parágrafos
> *What is the main difference between the approach in paragraph B and that in paragraph E?*
→ resuma B, resuma E, **compare**. A resposta não está numa frase só.

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: comparando parágrafos
> Paragraph **B** describes **the problem**, while paragraph **E** **suggests a solution**.
> → C / an early approach / F / a modern alternative → A / a common belief / D / evidence against it
### 4.2 Mini texto + pergunta integrada
> **A** In the 1950s and 60s, many American cities built large highways through their centres, believing this would bring economic growth. **B** Decades later, studies showed that these highways divided neighbourhoods and increased traffic rather than reducing it. **C** Since the 1990s, cities such as Seoul and San Francisco have removed some urban highways and replaced them with parks or boulevards. Contrary to predictions, traffic did not become worse; in some cases, it decreased.
**Pergunta:** *How did the results in paragraph C compare with the beliefs in paragraph A?*

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: texto misto (15 min — meta: 13 min de leitura e resposta)
> **The 15-Minute City**
> **A** The idea of the “15-minute city” has become popular among urban planners. It proposes that residents should be able to reach most daily needs — work, shops, schools, healthcare and parks — within a fifteen-minute walk or bike ride from home.
> **B** The concept is associated with Carlos Moreno, a professor at the Sorbonne in Paris, who argued that decades of car-centred planning had separated homes from workplaces and services, forcing people into long commutes.
> **C** Paris has adopted elements of the model, adding bike lanes, converting some streets into pedestrian zones and opening schoolyards to the public at weekends. Melbourne, in Australia, has a similar plan based on “20-minute neighbourhoods”.
> **D** Critics raise several concerns. Some argue that the idea works best in dense, wealthy city centres and may be harder to apply in suburbs, where homes are far apart. Others worry that improving local amenities could raise property prices and push lower-income residents out.
> **E** Supporters respond that the model is not a fixed rule but a set of goals that can be adapted to each place. They also point out that mixed-use zoning — allowing homes, shops and offices in the same area — was common in many cities before the twentieth century.
> **F** Whether the 15-minute city will become the norm remains uncertain. What is clear is that the pandemic, by forcing many people to stay close to home, made the idea more attractive to the public than ever before.
**Questions 1–3: True / False / Not Given**
1. The 15-minute city aims to give access to daily needs within a short walk or bike ride.
2. Carlos Moreno was the mayor of Paris.
3. Melbourne's plan uses a different time target from Paris.
**Questions 4–6: Which paragraph contains…?**
4. a historical precedent for the idea ___ 5. a possible negative economic effect ___ 6. examples of practical changes in a city ___
**Questions 7–8: Multiple choice**
7. *Why does the writer mention the pandemic?* A) to show it made the idea more popular B) to criticise lockdowns C) to explain Moreno's theory
8. *What is the main difference between paragraphs D and E?* A) D presents criticisms; E answers them B) D describes Paris; E describes Melbourne C) both support the idea
