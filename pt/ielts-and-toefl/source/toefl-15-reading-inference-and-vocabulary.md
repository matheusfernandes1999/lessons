## Objetivo da aula
**Foco:** perguntas de **inferência** (o que o texto sugere, mas não diz diretamente) e de **vocabulário em contexto** (o sentido de uma palavra naquela frase).
**Ao final da aula você consegue:** fazer inferências **justificáveis** — apoiadas no texto, sem “viajar” — e deduzir o sentido de palavras desconhecidas.
**Tarefa verificável:** resolver seis questões inferenciais e apontar evidência.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)
O professor lê pequenas situações; o aluno diz o que pode concluir.
- *“Maria is wearing a coat and holding an umbrella.”* → It's probably cold and rainy.
- *“The restaurant is empty on a Saturday night.”* → ______
- *“Paulo checked his phone five times during the meeting.”* → ______
**Frases úteis:** *It probably means…* · *We can infer that…* · *This suggests that…* · *I'm not sure, but…*
**Revisão da aula 14:** explique a técnica pergunta → palavra-chave → trecho → paráfrase.

## 2. Vocabulário-chave (4 min)
Linguagem de inferência:
- **infer** — inferir · **imply / suggest** — sugerir, dar a entender
- **probably / likely / unlikely** — provavelmente / provável / improvável
- **conclude** — concluir · **assume** — supor
- **support (a conclusion)** — sustentar · **evidence** — evidência
- **contrary to** — ao contrário de · **rather than** — em vez de
Pistas para vocabulário em contexto:
- **definição:** *X, which is…* / *X, or…* / *X — that is,…*
- **exemplo:** *such as, including, for example*
- **contraste:** *but, unlike, whereas* (sentido oposto)
- **sinônimo:** a mesma ideia repetida com outra palavra

### Pronúncia: ouça, compare e repita
- **infer / in fur / inform** — in-FER (tônica no fim) · **IN-fer-ence** (tônica no começo!)
- **imply / employ / apply** — im-PLY, em-PLOY, a-PPLY
- **assume / resume / consume** — as-SUME, re-SUME, con-SUME
Frase: > We can infer that the author assumes the reader knows the theory.

## 3. Explicação e modelo (4 min)
### Inferência boa × inferência ruim
- **Boa:** segue **logicamente** de uma frase do texto. Você consegue apontar a frase.
- **Ruim:** é verdade no mundo real, é opinião sua, ou é exagerada (*always, never, all*).
### Modelo
> Before the 1900s, most ice used in American cities was cut from frozen lakes in winter and stored in buildings insulated with sawdust. By the 1930s, however, the natural ice industry had almost disappeared.
**Pergunta:** *What can be inferred about the natural ice industry?*
- a) It was replaced by another technology. ✔ — “almost disappeared” + época (1930s) → refrigeração. É a inferência mais segura.
- b) Ice from lakes was dirty. ✘ — o texto não fala de qualidade.
- c) Nobody used ice after 1930. ✘ — exagero (“nobody”).
**Vocabulário em contexto:** *insulated with sawdust* → buildings that keep ice cold → *insulated* = isolado termicamente.

## 4. Prática guiada (10 min)
### 4.1 Troque e fale: justificando inferências
> We can infer that **the industry was replaced**, because the text says **it “had almost disappeared” by the 1930s**.
> → the café is not popular / it is empty on Saturday nights → the author prefers plan B / he calls plan A “costly and slow” → the experiment failed / the researchers “had to start again”

### 4.2 Adivinhe a palavra pela pista
1. The medicine **alleviated** her pain; she felt much better in an hour.
2. The town is **arid**; it rarely rains, and few plants grow.
3. Unlike his **frugal** brother, who saves every cent, Tom spends money freely.
4. Many **perishable** foods, such as milk and fish, must be kept cold.
5. The results were **ambiguous** — they could be interpreted in two different ways.

### 4.3 Boa ou ruim?
Texto: *“Although the new phone sold well in its first month, sales fell sharply after reviewers reported battery problems.”*
1. Battery problems probably affected sales. 2. The phone was the best on the market. 3. All customers returned their phones. 4. Reviews can influence buyers.

## 5. Tarefa cronometrada: 6 inferências (15 min)
### A. Leia — 3 min
> **The Dabbawalas of Mumbai**
> (1) Every day in Mumbai, around 5,000 delivery workers known as dabbawalas carry approximately 200,000 home-cooked lunches from homes to offices. The system began in the 1890s, when a migrant worker started delivering food to office employees who preferred meals from home.
> (2) Remarkably, most dabbawalas have limited formal education, and the system uses almost no technology. Each lunchbox is marked with a simple code of colors, numbers and letters that indicates its origin, the stations it passes through, and its destination. Boxes are sorted and re-sorted at train stations, often changing hands three or four times.
> (3) Despite this complexity, errors are extremely rare. Business schools have studied the dabbawalas, and some companies have invited them to give talks on teamwork and punctuality. Their success is often attributed to strong group identity, strict discipline, and a shared sense of responsibility.
> (4) In recent years, the system has faced challenges, including the rise of food-delivery apps and changes in work habits. Nevertheless, many families in Mumbai continue to rely on dabbawalas.

### B. Responda — 8 min
1. What can be inferred about office workers in the 1890s? 
2. What does paragraph 2 suggest about the coding system?
3. In paragraph 2, “sorted” is closest in meaning to: a) organized into groups b) cooked c) sold
4. Why have business schools studied the dabbawalas?
5. In paragraph 3, “attributed to” is closest in meaning to: a) paid for b) explained by c) hidden from
6. What can be inferred from paragraph 4? a) The system will end soon. b) Technology is a competitor, but the service is still valued. c) Families prefer apps.
**Acertos:** __/6

### C. Evidência — 4 min
Para cada resposta, aponte a frase do texto e diga: *“This suggests…, because the text says…”*
