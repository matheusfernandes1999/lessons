A. Overview: Why International Arbitration Matters

International arbitration is a preferred method for resolving cross-border business disputes, providing:

✅ Neutrality – Avoids national courts that may favor one party.
✅ Flexibility – Allows parties to choose procedural rules and arbitrators.
✅ Enforceability – Arbitration awards are recognized under the New York Convention (1958).

For example:

  • A multinational company sues a foreign government for breach of contract.

  • Two corporations from different jurisdictions dispute a supply agreement.

  • A cross-border investment dispute arises under a Bilateral Investment Treaty (BIT).

2. Key Legal Terms in International Arbitration

A. Essential Arbitration Terminology

Term

Meaning

Example in Context

Arbitration Agreement

A clause in a contract requiring disputes to be resolved through arbitration

"The parties agree that all disputes shall be resolved by arbitration under ICC rules."

Governing Law Clause

Specifies which jurisdiction’s laws apply to the contract

"This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of England and Wales."

Seat of Arbitration

The legal jurisdiction where arbitration proceedings are conducted

"The seat of arbitration shall be Singapore."

Arbitration Award

The final decision issued by the arbitrators

"The tribunal issued an arbitration award in favor of the claimant."

Bilateral Investment Treaty (BIT)

A treaty between two countries protecting investors

"The dispute arose under the US-Argentina BIT."

New York Convention (1958)

International treaty for enforcing arbitration awards

"The award was recognized under the New York Convention."

B. Common Clauses in Arbitration Agreements

Clause

Purpose

Example in Context

Arbitration Clause

Requires disputes to be settled via arbitration instead of court

"All disputes arising out of this contract shall be submitted to binding arbitration."

Choice of Law Clause

Specifies the legal system governing the contract

"This Agreement shall be interpreted under Swiss law."

Seat of Arbitration Clause

Determines the legal jurisdiction where arbitration is held

"The arbitration shall take place in Hong Kong."

Language Clause

Establishes the official language of arbitration

"Proceedings shall be conducted in English."

3. Challenges in Translating Arbitration Documents

A. Literal vs. Functional Translation

  • Legal concepts may not have direct equivalents in other languages.

  • Differences between common law and civil law arbitration practices.

  • Ambiguous translation may lead to unenforceability of arbitration awards.

English Term

Literal Translation (Problematic)

Functional Translation (Better)

Final and Binding Award

Decisão final e obrigatória

Sentença arbitral definitiva e executável

Enforcement of Award

Cumprimento da decisão arbitral

Execução da sentença arbitral conforme convenção aplicável

Competence-Competence Principle

Princípio da competência-competência

Poder do tribunal arbitral para decidir sobre sua própria jurisdição

B. Avoiding Ambiguity in Arbitration Translation

  • Unclear arbitration clauses can lead to litigation over validity.

  • Failure to specify procedural details may delay dispute resolution.

  • Misinterpretation of enforcement provisions can affect award recognition.
