A. Overview: Why Practical Translation Matters

Legal translation is not just about language skills—it requires an understanding of legal systems, terminology, and precision. A poorly translated contract can lead to legal disputes, and an ambiguous statute can result in misinterpretation in court.

For example:

  • A contract clause with vague language can be unenforceable.

  • A mistranslated statute can lead to incorrect legal interpretation.

  • A legal notice with an ambiguous deadline may cause compliance issues.

2. Translation Exercise 1: Contract Clauses

📖 Instruction: Translate the following contract clauses, ensuring clarity and legal enforceability.

Text 1: Excerpt from a Commercial Contract

"The Seller shall deliver the Goods within thirty (30) days from the Effective Date. If the Seller fails to do so, the Buyer may, at its discretion, terminate this Agreement and seek damages."

🔍 Key Considerations for Translation:

  1. Use precise legal terminology (e.g., “shall” vs. “may”).

  2. Ensure consistency in terminology (e.g., "Goods" should be capitalized if a defined term).

  3. Adapt for jurisdictional differences (e.g., UK vs. US contract language).

4. Translation Exercise 3: Legal Notices

📖 Instruction: Translate the following legal notice into a clear, authoritative format.

Text 3: Excerpt from a Notice of Breach

Pursuant to Clause 8 of the Lease Agreement, we hereby notify you that failure to remit payment within ten (10) days shall constitute a material breach of contract. Without prejudice to our rights, we reserve the right to initiate legal proceedings to recover all outstanding amounts.

5. Common Legal Translation Mistakes & How to Fix Them

A. Common Pitfalls in Legal Translation

Error Type

Problem

Example

Correction

False Cognates

Words that look similar but have different legal meanings

"Execution" (contract signing vs. capital punishment)

Use "signing" for contracts

Over-Literal Translation

Direct translation that distorts legal meaning

"Contract will be nulo e vazio."

"Contract will be void."

Ambiguity

Unclear meaning due to improper phrasing

"The company must follow applicable rules."

"The company must comply with all regulations under [specific law]."

Passive Voice

Makes responsibility unclear

"The contract was breached."

"The Supplier breached the contract."
