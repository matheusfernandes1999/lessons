2. Key Legal Terms Used in Trials

A. Essential Courtroom Terminology

Term

Meaning

Example in Context

Opening Statement

Initial argument outlining each side’s case

"The prosecution delivered a compelling opening statement outlining the charges."

Direct Examination

Questioning of a witness by the party that called them

"During direct examination, the witness confirmed the timeline of events."

Cross-Examination

Questioning of a witness by the opposing side

"The defense cross-examined the expert witness to challenge their credibility."

Objection

A lawyer’s protest against improper testimony or procedure

"Objection, Your Honor! Leading question."

Sustained

The judge agrees with the objection, and the question is withdrawn

"Objection sustained. The jury will disregard that statement."

Overruled

The judge disagrees with the objection, and the question remains

"Objection overruled. The witness must answer the question."

Closing Argument

Final argument summarizing the case

"The defense’s closing argument emphasized the lack of evidence."

Burden of Proof

The obligation to prove a claim (on the prosecution in criminal cases)

"The prosecution has the burden of proof to establish guilt beyond a reasonable doubt."

🔍 Exercise: Identify whether each term is used by the judge, prosecution, defense, or witnesses.

B. Common Objections in Court

Objection

Reason for Use

Example in Context

Leading Question

The question suggests the answer

"Objection! The prosecution is leading the witness."

Hearsay

Second-hand testimony that is not firsthand knowledge

"Objection! The witness is repeating something they heard from someone else."

Relevance

The testimony does not relate to the case

"Objection! The question is not relevant to the facts of the case."

Speculation

The witness is guessing instead of stating facts

"Objection! The witness is speculating about the defendant’s motives."

3. Structure of a Mock Trial

A. Trial Stages

  1. Opening Statements – Prosecution and defense introduce their arguments.

  2. Prosecution’s Case – Prosecution presents evidence and examines witnesses.

  3. Defense’s Case – Defense cross-examines witnesses and presents counterarguments.

  4. Closing Arguments – Both sides summarize their positions.

  5. Jury Deliberation & Verdict – Jury or judge decides the case.

🔍 Exercise: Assign roles (judge, prosecution, defense, witnesses, jury) for a mock trial.

B. Mock Trial Case Scenario

📖 Case Study: The People vs. John Smith

Case Summary:
John Smith is accused of grand theft for allegedly stealing a luxury car from a dealership. The prosecution claims security footage shows Smith taking the car after hours. The defense argues that Smith had permission to use the vehicle and that the footage is inconclusive.

🔍 Roles:

  • Judge: Presides over the trial and rules on objections.

  • Prosecution: Presents evidence against Smith.

  • Defense: Challenges the evidence and protects Smith’s rights.

  • Witnesses: Provide testimony for both sides.

  • Jury: Decides the verdict based on the evidence.

4. Mock Trial Simulation: Role-Playing Activity

📖 Instruction: Each participant takes on a role in the trial and follows courtroom procedure.

A. Opening Statements

✅ Example – Prosecution:
"Ladies and gentlemen of the jury, the evidence will show that the defendant, John Smith, was caught on security footage driving a stolen vehicle away from the dealership at midnight. The dealership never authorized him to take the car. We will prove beyond a reasonable doubt that he is guilty of grand theft."

✅ Example – Defense:
"Members of the jury, my client, John Smith, had permission to take the vehicle, as we will demonstrate through witness testimony. The prosecution’s case is based on assumptions and misleading evidence. There is no proof of criminal intent."

B. Witness Testimony & Cross-Examination

Example – Direct Examination (Prosecution & Witness):

Prosecutor: "Did you see the defendant inside the dealership after closing hours?"
Witness: "Yes, I saw him leaving with the car around midnight."

Example – Cross-Examination (Defense & Witness):

Defense Attorney: "Did you personally hear anyone from the dealership deny him permission?"
Witness: "No, I just assumed he didn't have permission."

C. Closing Arguments

✅ Example – Prosecution:
"The evidence clearly shows that John Smith took the vehicle without permission. The security footage, witness statements, and dealership records confirm that this was an unauthorized act. We ask that you return a verdict of guilty."

✅ Example – Defense:
"The prosecution has failed to provide concrete proof that my client stole the vehicle. The footage is unclear, and there is no direct evidence showing that he lacked permission. We ask for a verdict of not guilty."

🔍 Exercise: Each participant delivers their final statement.

5. Jury Deliberation & Verdict Discussion

🏛️ Activity: The jury discusses the case and delivers a verdict.

🔍 Questions for Jury Discussion:

  1. Did the prosecution meet its burden of proof?

  2. Did the defense raise reasonable doubt?

  3. What factors influenced the final verdict?

📚 Jury submits their decision and explains their reasoning.
