📘 O que são Adjectives and Modifiers?

🟡 Adjectives (Adjetivos)

Descrevem ou dão mais informações sobre os substantivos.
Ex: a big house, a happy student, an interesting book

Exemplos comuns:

  • big / small

  • good / bad

  • happy / sad

  • fast / slow

  • old / young

🔵 Comparatives (Comparativos)

Comparam duas coisas ou pessoas.

Fórmulas principais:

  • Adjetivos curtos: adjective + -er + than → smaller than, taller than, faster than

  • Adjetivos longos: more + adjective + than → more beautiful than, more interesting than

  • Irregulares: good → better, bad → worse, far → farther

Exemplos:

  • My car is faster than yours.

  • This book is more interesting than the movie.

  • She is better than me at soccer.

🟣 Opposites (Adjetivos opostos)

  • tall ↔ short

  • old ↔ young

  • easy ↔ difficult

  • expensive ↔ cheap

  • clean ↔ dirty

🔴 Adverbs (Advérbios)

Descrevem como, quando, onde ou com que frequência uma ação acontece.
Muitos adjetivos viram advérbios com “-ly”.

Exemplos:

  • slow → slowly

  • quick → quickly

  • careful → carefully

  • happy → happily

Frases:

  • He runs quickly.

  • She speaks softly.

  • I worked hard today.

🟢 "Like" em Comparações

A palavra like é usada para fazer comparações informais, especialmente na fala do dia a dia.

Exemplos:

  • She runs like a cheetah.

  • His singing is like an angel's.

  • They fought like gladiators in the arena.

Em contextos mais formais, é melhor usar:
She runs as fast as a cheetah. ou She is similar to a cheetah when she runs.

🟣 "Like" com Sentidos de Aparência, Som, Sabor, Cheiro e Situação

LOOK (aparência): Usado para indicar semelhança visual ou física entre pessoas ou coisas.

  • I look like my uncle.

  • She looks just like her mother.

  • He looked like his father.

  • I used to look like a young person.

  • This book could look better. (forma correta de: "could look like better")

SOUND (som): Refere-se à semelhança no som da voz, sotaque ou ruído.

  • This singer sounds like an old singer.

  • My brother sounds just like our grandpa.

  • I could sound a little bit like you if I trained.

  • What did it sound like? → It sounded just like a flat tire.

TASTE (sabor): Usado para indicar semelhança no gosto.

  • Did you taste the cake before buying?

  • This food doesn’t taste so good.

  • This soup tastes like childhood.

  • This tastes just like the food I ate yesterday.

SMELL (cheiro): Refere-se à comparação de cheiros.

  • This flower smells like honey.

  • The room smells just like my grandmother’s kitchen.

  • That perfume smells like vanilla.

SITUATION or FEELING (situação ou sensação): Pode ser usado para descrever contextos, sensações ou atmosferas similares.

  • It feels like winter already.

  • That sounds like a great idea.

  • This place feels like home.

  • Today feels like a holiday.

💡 Podemos intensificar a comparação com "just" → "just like...".

📖 Textos de Prática

My Two Brothers

I have two brothers. Tom is older and taller than Jack. Jack is more talkative and funnier. Tom studies hard and always wakes up early. Jack is more relaxed and walks slowly. They are very different, but I love them both.

Responda:

  • Who is taller?

  • Who is funnier?

  • How does Tom study?

  • Cite 2 comparativos no texto.

  • Cite 2 advérbios no texto.

🧪 Class Practice – Categorize Examples

Exemplos:

  • His laughter sounds like a hyena.

  • This painting looks like a masterpiece.

  • The coffee tastes just like the one I had yesterday.

  • She dances like a professional ballerina.

  • The cologne smells like fresh citrus.

Categorias:

  • A. Comparison

  • B. Appearance

  • C. Sound

  • D. Taste

📌 Revisão da Aula

  • Adjectives descrevem substantivos.

  • Comparatives comparam duas coisas.

  • Opposites ajudam a expandir vocabulário.

  • Adverbs mostram como algo acontece.

  • Like pode indicar comparação, aparência, som, gosto, cheiro ou sensação.

  • Muitos advérbios terminam em "-ly".
