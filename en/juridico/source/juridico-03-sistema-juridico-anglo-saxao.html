Grammar Point

Comparative Structures

Explanation:
When comparing legal systems or practices, comparative structures help us describe differences and similarities precisely. In legal contexts, we often use adjectives and adverbs with comparatives to highlight characteristics such as flexibility, rigidity, and reliance on precedent.

Key Structures & Examples:

  • Comparative Adjectives:

    • "Common Law is more flexible than Civil Law."

    • "The Anglo-Saxon system is less rigid than a codified system."

  • "As...as" Constructions:

    • "Judicial discretion in Common Law is as important as statutory guidance in Civil Law."

  • Using "than":

    • "Common Law relies more on precedent than on written codes."

2. Reading Practice

  • 📖 Instruction: Ask the student to read the following text aloud.

The Anglo-Saxon Legal System: Origins and Principles

The Anglo-Saxon legal system originated in early medieval England and laid the foundation for what is now known as Common Law. In this system, legal decisions are primarily based on judicial precedents rather than strictly written codes. Judges exercise significant discretion, allowing them to adapt legal principles to new situations. This flexibility contrasts with Civil Law systems, where judges apply detailed statutory provisions. As a result, understanding the Anglo-Saxon system is essential for legal professionals working in international contexts.
