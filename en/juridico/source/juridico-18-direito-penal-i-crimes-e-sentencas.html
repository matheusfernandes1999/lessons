Criminal Law – Crimes and Sentences

🎯 Objectives:

  1. Understand key criminal law terminology, including felony, misdemeanor, conviction, and acquittal.

  2. Differentiate between types of crimes and their legal consequences.

  3. Analyze criminal sentencing and the factors influencing court decisions.

  4. Apply criminal law terminology in reading, writing, and translation exercises.

1. Introduction

  • 📌 The session will cover:

  • The classification of crimes (felony vs. misdemeanor) and their punishments.

  • Key terms used in criminal trials and sentencing.

  • The difference between conviction, acquittal, and plea agreements.

  • The role of intent, negligence, and recklessness in criminal law.

  • Why Criminal Law Matters

  • Criminal law regulates behavior that is considered harmful to society and prescribes punishments for offenses. It is essential for legal professionals to understand:

  • How crimes are classified and prosecuted.

  • The rights of defendants and the burden of proof.

  • The range of legal penalties, from fines to life imprisonment.

  • For example:

  • A felony like armed robbery carries a severe punishment.

  • A misdemeanor like petty theft results in lighter penalties.

  • Sentencing varies depending on aggravating and mitigating factors.

  • ❓ Discussion Questions:

  • "What is the difference between a felony and a misdemeanor?"
    "Why do some criminal cases end in plea bargains instead of trials?"
    "What factors influence sentencing decisions in criminal cases?"

2. Key Legal Terms in Criminal Law

A. Classification of Crimes

Term

Meaning

Example in Context

Felony

A serious crime, usually punishable by imprisonment over 1 year

"Murder and fraud are considered felonies in most jurisdictions."

Misdemeanor

A less serious crime, punishable by fines or imprisonment under 1 year

"Shoplifting is a misdemeanor punishable by a fine."

Infraction

A minor violation, usually resulting in a fine

"Speeding is an infraction that does not lead to jail time."

White-Collar Crime

Non-violent financial crimes

"Embezzlement is a common white-collar crime."

Violent Crime

Crimes involving physical harm

"Assault and battery are categorized as violent crimes."

B. Criminal Proceedings and Sentencing

Term

Meaning

Example in Context

Conviction

A formal declaration that a defendant is guilty

"The defendant received a conviction for fraud."

Acquittal

A judgment that a defendant is not guilty

"The jury returned an acquittal due to lack of evidence."

Plea Bargain

An agreement where a defendant pleads guilty to a lesser charge

"He accepted a plea bargain to reduce his sentence."

Burden of Proof

The obligation to prove the defendant’s guilt

"In criminal cases, the burden of proof is beyond a reasonable doubt."

Sentencing

The judicial determination of a punishment

"The judge issued a five-year prison sentence."

C. Factors Influencing Sentencing

Factor

Impact on Sentence

Example in Context

Aggravating Factor

Increases severity of the sentence

"Using a firearm during a robbery is an aggravating factor."

Mitigating Factor

Reduces the severity of the sentence

"The defendant’s lack of criminal history was a mitigating factor."

Parole

Early release from prison under supervision

"He was granted parole after serving half of his sentence."

Probation

A sentence allowing supervised freedom instead of jail

"The judge sentenced her to probation instead of jail time."

Restitution

Compensation paid to victims by the offender

"The company was ordered to pay restitution for damages."

3. Reading Practice: Criminal Law in Context

  • 📖 Instruction: Read the following legal texts aloud and analyze the terminology used.

Text 1: Excerpt from a Criminal Court Ruling

"The defendant was convicted of grand larceny, a felony punishable by up to ten years in prison. The prosecution established guilt beyond a reasonable doubt, presenting clear evidence of intent. However, due to mitigating factors, including the defendant’s cooperation with authorities, the court imposed a reduced sentence of five years with eligibility for parole."

  • 🔍 Comprehension Questions:

  1. What was the defendant convicted of?

  2. What does “beyond a reasonable doubt” mean in this case?

  3. What mitigating factors influenced the sentence?

Text 2: Excerpt from a Plea Agreement

"The defendant agrees to plead guilty to a lesser charge of fraud in exchange for a reduced sentence. In accordance with the plea bargain, the prosecution recommends probation instead of incarceration. The agreement is subject to judicial approval."

  • 🔍 Comprehension Questions:

  1. What is the purpose of a plea bargain?

  2. Why might a defendant accept a plea deal?

  3. What role does the judge play in this agreement?

4. Writing Exercise: Drafting a Legal Statement

✍️ Prompt: Write a short sentencing statement using at least three legal terms from the lesson.

✅ Example Answer:

"The defendant is convicted of aggravated assault, a felony offense. The court finds that the use of a deadly weapon is an aggravating factor, warranting a sentence of ten years in prison. However, the defendant’s lack of prior criminal history serves as a mitigating factor, making him eligible for parole after five years."

Conclusion

✅ Sentencing decisions depend on aggravating and mitigating factors.
✅ Plea bargains help reduce court backlog but can be controversial.
