A. Overview: Why Liability and Negligence Matter

Liability and negligence are fundamental concepts in tort law, determining when a person or entity is legally responsible for harm caused to others.

For example:

  • A doctor may be liable for malpractice if they fail to follow professional standards.

  • A company may be held liable for defective products that cause injury.

  • A driver may be found negligent if they cause an accident by running a red light.

Understanding how courts determine fault and damages is essential for legal professionals, translators, and contract drafters working with liability clauses.

2. Key Legal Terms in Liability and Negligence

A. Essential Terms in Negligence and Liability Law

Term

Meaning

Example in Context

Duty of Care

Legal obligation to act reasonably to prevent harm

"A doctor owes a duty of care to their patients."

Breach of Duty

Failure to meet the required standard of care

"The driver’s excessive speed was a clear breach of duty."

Causation

A direct link between the defendant’s action and the harm suffered

"The plaintiff must prove causation to establish liability."

Foreseeability

Whether harm was predictable or preventable

"The company should have foreseen the destructive posed by the defective product."

Strict Liability

Legal responsibility without proof of negligence

"Manufacturers are strictly liable for harm caused by defective products."

Comparative Negligence

A system where fault is divided between parties

"The court reduced damages due to the plaintiff’s comparative negligence."

B. Key Elements of a Negligence Claim

To prove negligence, a plaintiff must establish the following:

  1. Duty of Care – Did the defendant have a legal obligation to act responsibly?

  2. Breach of Duty – Did the defendant fail to meet this obligation?

  3. Causation – Did the breach directly cause harm?

  4. Damages – Did the plaintiff suffer measurable harm?

A. Literal vs. Functional Translation

  • Negligence and liability standards differ by jurisdiction, requiring careful adaptation.

  • Legal terminology must be precisely translated to avoid ambiguity.

  • Cultural and legal system differences can affect the meaning of legal terms.

B. Avoiding Ambiguity in Negligence and Liability Cases

  • Vague language can weaken legal arguments in liability disputes.

  • Overuse of passive voice can obscure responsibility (e.g., "The injury was caused" vs. "The defendant caused the injury.").

  • Key legal concepts may not have direct translations, requiring explanation.

4. Reading Practice: Case Law on Negligence and Liability

📖 Instruction: Read the following legal excerpts aloud and analyze the legal reasoning used.

Text 1: Excerpt from a Court Ruling on Negligence

"The defendant, a property owner, had a legal duty to maintain the premises in a safe condition. Evidence presented shows that the stairwell lighting had been non-functional for several weeks, despite multiple complaints. The court finds that this failure to act constitutes a breach of duty. Furthermore, the plaintiff’s injuries were a direct result of this hazard, satisfying the element of causation. Therefore, the defendant is liable for damages."

🔍 Comprehension Questions:

  3. What evidence supported causation in this ruling?

Text 2: Excerpt from a Product Liability Case

"The plaintiff suffered severe burns due to a defective heating pad sold by the defendant. Under strict liability principles, a manufacturer is responsible for harm caused by a defective product, regardless of intent or negligence. The court rules that the product was unreasonably destructiveous and that no adequate warning was provided. Accordingly, the defendant is liable for damages."

🔍 Comprehension Questions:

  1. How does strict liability differ from regular negligence?

  2. Why does the court rule in favor of the plaintiff, even without proof of negligence?

  3. How might the lack of an adequate warning impact liability?

5. Writing Exercise: Drafting a Legal Argument on Negligence

✍️ Prompt: Write a short legal argument applying negligence principles, using at least three legal terms from today’s lesson.

✅ Example Answer:

"The plaintiff contends that the defendant breached their duty of care by failing to repair the broken stairwell. Given that multiple complaints were made and no corrective action was taken, this constitutes gross negligence. As the plaintiff’s injuries were a direct and foreseeable result of this hazardous condition, causation is satisfied. Therefore, the defendant is liable for damages under premises liability principles."
