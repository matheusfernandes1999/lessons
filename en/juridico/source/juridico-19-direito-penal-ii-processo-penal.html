Criminal Procedure – Trials and Evidence

🎯 Objectives:

  1. Understand key criminal procedure terminology, including habeas corpus, burden of proof, and due process.

  2. Identify the stages of a criminal trial, from arrest to sentencing.

  3. Analyze the role of evidence and how it impacts a trial.

  4. Apply criminal procedure terminology in reading, writing, and translation exercises.

1. Introduction

  • 📌 The session will cover:

  • The criminal trial process and legal principles governing it.

  • The types of evidence and their impact on a case.

  • Key legal protections, including due process and habeas corpus.

  • The distinction between direct and circumstantial evidence.

  • Why Criminal Procedure Matters

  • Criminal procedure defines the rules and protections for individuals accused of crimes. It ensures that trials are fair, evidence is reliable, and legal rights are upheld.

  • For example:

  • The burden of proof is on the prosecution to prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

  • Illegally obtained evidence may be excluded from trial.

  • Defendants have rights such as legal representation and a fair trial.

  • ❓ Discussion Questions:

  • "Why is due process important in criminal trials?"
    "What happens if evidence is obtained illegally?"
    "What is the difference between direct and circumstantial evidence?"

2. Key Legal Terms in Criminal Procedure

A. Trial Process and Legal Protections

Term

Meaning

Example in Context

Due Process

The right to fair legal procedures

"The defendant argued that his due process rights were violated."

Habeas Corpus

A legal action challenging unlawful detention

"The prisoner filed a habeas corpus petition to challenge his imprisonment."

Burden of Proof

The obligation to prove guilt

"In criminal cases, the burden of proof lies with the prosecution."

Presumption of Innocence

The principle that defendants are innocent until proven guilty

"The presumption of innocence is a fundamental right in criminal law."

Double Jeopardy

Protection against being tried twice for the same offense

"The defendant cannot face double jeopardy after being acquitted."

B. Stages of a Criminal Trial

Stage

Description

Example in Context

Arrest

The suspect is taken into custody

"The police arrested the suspect based on probable cause."

Indictment

A formal charge issued against a defendant

"The grand jury issued an indictment for fraud."

Arraignment

The defendant is formally charged and enters a plea

"During the arraignment, he pleaded not guilty."

Trial

The prosecution and defense present their cases

"The trial lasted three weeks and included witness testimony."

Verdict

The jury or judge decides guilt or innocence

"The jury returned a guilty verdict."

Sentencing

The judge determines the punishment

"The defendant received a ten-year sentence."

C. Types of Evidence in Criminal Trials

Type of Evidence

Definition

Example in Context

Direct Evidence

Evidence that directly proves a fact

"The security footage showing the crime is direct evidence."

Circumstantial Evidence

Indirect evidence that suggests a fact

"The defendant's fingerprints on the weapon were circumstantial evidence."

Hearsay

Secondhand testimony that is generally inadmissible

"The court ruled the witness's hearsay testimony inadmissible."

Forensic Evidence

Scientific evidence (DNA, fingerprints)

"Forensic evidence linked the suspect to the crime scene."

Testimonial Evidence

Statements made by witnesses under oath

"The victim’s testimony was key to the conviction."
