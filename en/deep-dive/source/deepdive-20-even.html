🎯 Overview

The word “even” in English is a powerful tool used to express surprise, emphasis, contrast, or unexpectedness. It does not translate directly to one single word in Portuguese — it may mean até, mesmo, nem, or have no translation at all.

Examples:

  • Even she didn’t know what to say. (Surprise)

  • He didn’t even call me back. (Emphasis)

  • It’s cold even though it’s summer. (Contrast)

We use “even” to:

  • Emphasize unexpected information

  • Show something is more extreme or surprising than normal

  • Highlight contrast (even though)

  • Reinforce comparisons

🔍 Deep Dive

1. Positions of "even"

  • Before the word it emphasizes:

    • Even my dog liked the movie.

  • Before auxiliary verbs or modals in negative sentences:

    • She didn’t even say goodbye.

  • Not usually at the beginning of a sentence unless part of even though.

2. “Even though” vs “Even if”

Expression

Meaning

Example

Even though

Contrast with a real fact

Even though it’s raining, we’re going out.

Even if

Contrast with a possible condition

Even if it rains, we’ll go out.

⚠️ Common Portuguese speaker mistake:
He even not arrived yet. ❌
Correct: He hasn’t even arrived yet. ✅
(Positioning is key!)

3. Mini Dialogues


A: She didn’t come to the meeting.

A: You’re going to Paris too?
B: Yeah, and even Rome!


A: You failed the easiest part!
B: I know! I didn’t even see that question.


A: You’re still going out?
B: Yeah, even though I’m exhausted.

🔁 Deep Review

  • “Even” adds emphasis, contrast, or surprise.

  • It has flexible positioning but must be placed carefully.

  • Watch out for false friends like “mesmo”, “até”, or “nem”.

  • Know the difference between “even though” and “even if”.
