🎯 Overview

What are numbers in English?

Numbers are not just for math — they’re essential in daily communication: dates, time, prices, documents, addresses, and more.

Key types of numbers:

  • Cardinal: one, two, three…

  • Ordinal: first, second, third…

  • Fractions: one-half, three-quarters…

  • Percentages: ten percent (10%)

  • Time: three o’clock, half past two

  • Dates: the 5th of May, May 5th

  • Phone numbers: 555-6789

  • Document/ID numbers: CPF: zero-two-four, eight-seven…

Why do we use them?
To give accurate information, describe sequences, talk about statistics, or share personal/contact info.

Examples:

  • “My birthday is on the 14th of July.”

  • “She scored ninety-five percent on the test.”

  • “Call me at (11) 98888-1234.”

🔍 Deep Dive

Let’s go deeper into the different types of numbers and how to use them naturally:

🧮 1. Cardinal Numbers – Quantity

  • Used for counting and stating amounts.

  • Examples: one, two, three, four...

Example sentences:

  • “There are five people in the room.”

  • “He owns three houses.”

🥇 2. Ordinal Numbers – Position/Order

  • first, second, third, fourth, etc.

  • Used in addresses, dates, sequences.

Common mistakes:
❌ “The seven day”
✅ “The seventh day”

Example:

  • “I live on the ninth floor.”

  • “His birthday is on the twenty-third of June.”

➗ 3. Fractions and Decimals

  • Fractions: “one-half”, “three-quarters”

  • Decimals: “three point five” (3.5)

Example:

  • “He ran two and a half kilometers.”

  • “My phone has 4.7 inches of screen.”

📈 4. Percentages

  • “percent” is used for proportions: 20%, 45.5%

Example:

  • “Only 30% of the group finished on time.”

🕒 5. Time

  • Analog: “quarter past three”, “half past six”

  • Digital: “six thirty”, “four fifteen”

Example:

  • “The meeting is at ten to five.” (4:50)

📅 6. Dates

  • British: “the 5th of May”

  • American: “May 5th”

Pronounced: “May fifth”, “the fifth of May”

☎️ 7. Phone and Document Numbers

  • Spoken digit by digit (e.g., 123 → one-two-three)

  • For repeated numbers: double/triple (UK/US: optional)

Example:

  • “Call me at (21) 99888-7766 — that’s nine-nine-eight-eight-eight double seven six six.”

Real-life mini dialogues:

At the doctor’s:
A: “What’s your CPF number?”
B: “Zero-two-four, five-seven-eight, nine-zero-zero.”

Booking a meeting:
A: “What day works for you?”
B: “Let’s do the 23rd of March, at ten thirty.”

Shopping:
A: “That’s twelve dollars and fifty cents.”
B: “Here’s twenty.”

Comprehension Questions:

  2. What percentage did they score on the test?

  3. What is the deadline for the project?

🔁 Deep Review

Main Takeaways:

  • Cardinal = quantity (one, two, three)

  • Ordinal = order (first, second, third)

  • Fractions/Decimals = common in measurement

  • Percentages = statistics, evaluations

  • Time/Dates = everyday use, various formats

  • Phone/ID Numbers = always spelled out clearly
