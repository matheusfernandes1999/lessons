🎯 Overview

What are nouns?

Nouns are words that name people, places, things, ideas, or feelings.

Examples:

  • teacher, Brazil, happiness, information, cat

At an advanced level, we go beyond basic usage and look into:

  • Formation: create nouns from verbs/adjectives (decide → decision)

  • Irregular plurals: man → men, analysis → analyses

  • Countable vs. uncountable nuances

  • Abstract vs. concrete nouns

  • Compound nouns: mother-in-law, checkout

Why it matters:

In Portuguese, many of these transformations and distinctions are handled differently. Recognizing English noun patterns improves fluency and prevents errors in writing, listening, and advanced reading comprehension.

🔍 Deep Dive

1. Noun Formation

Many English nouns come from verbs or adjectives:

Verb

Noun

Example

decide

decision

I made a tough decision.

choose

choice

You have no other choice.

arrive

arrival

His arrival was delayed.

improve

improvement

There’s been a big improvement.

Adjective

Noun

Example

happy

happiness

True happiness is rare.

strong

strength

She has great mental strength.

🧠 Portuguese trap: information is uncountable in English (not "informations").

2. Irregular Plural Nouns

Singular

Plural

man

men

woman

women

child

children

tooth

teeth

foot

feet

person

people

analysis

analyses

crisis

crises

phenomenon

phenomena

🧠 Portuguese speakers may default to adding “-s” to all nouns. Be careful!

Examples in context:

  • These analyses are quite complex.

  • There were three women at the table.

  • I’ve read several theses on this topic.

3. Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

Countable

Uncountable

book, apple, idea

advice, news, equipment

⚠️ In English, you say:

  • some advice ✅

  • a piece of furniture ✅

  • a news ❌

🧠 Watch out: Some nouns that are countable in Portuguese are uncountable in English.

4. Abstract vs. Concrete Nouns

  • Freedom, happiness, knowledge (abstract)

  • Chair, phone, cat (concrete)

Using abstract nouns well improves formal writing.

5. Compound Nouns

These are made of two or more words acting as one noun.

Examples:

  • mother-in-law

  • checkout

  • dry cleaner

  • coffee shop

🧠 Plural form is tricky:

  • mothers-in-law ✅

  • coffee shops ✅

👥 Real-life Dialogues

1. Academic setting:
A: How many analyses did you run?
B: Five. Each one gave a different result.

2. Workplace:
A: I need some advice on this presentation.
B: Sure! I’ll give you a few suggestions.

3. Personal life:
A: That was a tough decision to make.
B: But it was the right choice.

4. Shopping trip:
A: The checkout line is too long.
B: Let’s try the other cashier.
