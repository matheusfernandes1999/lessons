🎯 Overview

What are adverbs?

Adverbs describe how, when, where, or to what degree an action is done. They often modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

Adverb Formation

Most adverbs are formed by adding -ly to an adjective.

Examples:

  • quick → quickly

  • happy → happily

  • careful → carefully

Why is it important?

Forming adverbs correctly allows you to describe actions with clarity, precision, and natural fluency — especially in storytelling, arguments, or formal writing.

🔍 Deep Dive

1. Regular Formation: Adjective + -ly

Adjective

Adverb

slow

slowly

loud

loudly

honest

honestly

lucky

luckily

2. Spelling Rules

  • Adjectives ending in -y: change y to i + ly
    happy → happily
    angry → angrily

  • Adjectives ending in -le: change -e to -y
    simple → simply
    terrible → terribly

  • Adjectives ending in -ic: add -ally
    basic → basically
    tragic → tragically

3. Irregular Adverbs (Same as adjective)

Some adverbs look exactly like adjectives.

Adjective/Adverb

Example

fast

He runs fast.

hard

She works hard.

late

They arrived late.

early

We left early.

⚠️ “Hardly” = almost not at all (not the same as “hard”)
→ I hardly know him. (Quase não o conheço.)

4. Mini Dialogues

1 – Interview
A: Did he explain the concept well?
B: Yes, he spoke clearly and logically.

2 – Accident
A: What happened?
B: He was driving too fast, and he reacted slowly.

3 – Presentation
A: Did she present well?
B: She started confidently, but finished nervously.

4 – Reaction
A: How did they take the news?
B: Surprisingly, they weren’t upset at all.

🔁 Deep Review

Summary:

  • Most adverbs are formed by adding -ly to an adjective.

  • Watch out for spelling changes: happy → happily, terrible → terribly.

  • Some adverbs are irregular and don’t use “-ly” at all: fast, hard, early.

  • “Hardly” is not the same as “hard”!

  • Use adverbs to describe actions more naturally and professionally.
