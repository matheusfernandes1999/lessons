🎯 Overview


The word "to" is extremely common in English, but it serves many roles that go beyond just expressing movement (e.g., "go to the store").
It can express purpose (I came to help), infinitive verbs (to learn, to speak), relationships (married to, similar to), and even comparison or preference (prefer tea to coffee).

Examples:

  • I’m going to the beach. (Direction)

  • She wants to travel. (Infinitive)

  • He’s married to a doctor. (Relationship)

We use "to" to:

  • Show direction or destination

  • Introduce the infinitive form of a verb

  • Express a relationship or connection

  • Indicate preference or comparison

  • Introduce the recipient of an action

🔍 Deep Dive

1. "To" + Infinitive Verb

  • Used to express intention, plan, or desire

    • I hope to visit Brazil next year.

    • He agreed to come with us.

    • She refused to help.

Note: In Portuguese, we often say just the verb: Ela quer sair → She wants to leave (don't drop the to in English!).

2. "To" for Direction or Destination

  • Standard use for physical or metaphorical direction

    • We walked to the hotel.

    • This leads to a big misunderstanding.

3. "To" in Relationships

  • With adjectives: married to, loyal to, nice to

    • She’s married to a teacher.

    • They were kind to us.

    • He’s allergic to peanuts.

⚠️Common mistake for Portuguese speakers:
“Casado com” = “Married to” (not “with”)

4. "To" for Comparison or Preference

  • With verbs like prefer, compare, relate, similar

    • I prefer tea to coffee.

    • This is similar to what we discussed.

5. "To" as Purpose/Result

  • Often used in place of “in order to”

    • He studies to pass the test.

    • I worked hard to succeed.

Mini Dialogues:
A: Why do you study English every day?
B: To improve my fluency, of course!

A: Are you going to the concert tonight?
B: Yes, and I’m taking my brother to celebrate his birthday.

A: She’s married to an American, right?
B: Yes. That’s why she moved to New York.

🔁 Deep Review

Summary of Key Points:

  • “To” is used with infinitive verbs (to go, to study).

  • It shows direction, purpose, or intention.

  • It marks relationship (married to, similar to).

  • It appears in comparisons (prefer X to Y).

  • Portuguese often omits or replaces “to” – careful!
