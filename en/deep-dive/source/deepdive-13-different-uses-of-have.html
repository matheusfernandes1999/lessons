🎯 Overview

What does have really mean?

At first glance, have seems easy — it means “ter”, right? But have goes far beyond possession. In English, have also expresses:

  • Possession: I have a car.

  • Obligation: I have to go.

  • Experiences: I’ve had lunch.

  • States/Conditions: I have a headache.

  • Causative: I had my hair cut.

  • Collocations: have fun, have a break, have a nap

Why is this tricky for Brazilians?

In Portuguese, we use “ter”, “estar com”, and other verbs instead of have in some contexts. For example:

  • Estou com fome → I am hungry (❌ I have hunger)

  • Eu tenho que ir → I have to go ✅

  • Eu já almocei → I have had lunch ✅

Understanding have properly helps avoid literal translations and boosts your fluency.

🔍 Deep Dive

📌 Key Uses of Have

1. Possession (ter)

  • I have a brother.

  • They don’t have a car.

🧠 Tip: Use have or have got (mainly in British English):
I’ve got a new phone.

2. Obligation (ter que)

  • I have to finish this report.

  • She has to study more.

🧠 Structure:
have to + verb (base form)
He has to go. / Do you have to leave now?

3. Experience or Completion (Present Perfect)

  • I’ve had three coffees today.

  • Have you ever had sushi?

🧠 Structure:


have/has + past participle

🧠 Portuguese equivalent:
Eu já comi. = I’ve eaten.

4. States and Conditions

  • I have a headache.

  • He has a cold.

🧠 In Portuguese, we often say “estar com dor”, but in English, we use have.

5. Collocations (common phrases)

  • have breakfast/lunch/dinner

  • have fun

  • have a nap

  • have a meeting

  • have a conversation

  • have a drink

🧠 Collocations sound natural — memorize them as chunks!

6. Causative Structure

  • I had my hair done yesterday.

  • She had her car washed.

🧠 Structure: have + object + past participle
Meaning: someone else did it for you.

👥 Real-life Dialogues

1. Small talk:
A: Did you have a good weekend?
B: Yes, I had a great time with my family.

2. At the doctor’s:
A: What seems to be the problem?
B: I have a terrible headache and a sore throat.

3. Work meeting:
A: I have to leave early today.
B: No worries, just send me the report before you go.

4. Daily routine:
A: Have you had lunch?
B: Not yet, I’m starving.

5. Salon appointment:
A: Your hair looks great!
B: Thanks! I had it cut this morning.

🔁 Deep Review

Key Takeaways:

  • Have ≠ just “ter” — it also means “passar por”, “sofrer”, “sentir”, “fazer”, “comer” and more.

  • Watch out for false translations:
    Estou com dor = I have a pain (not I am with pain)

  • Learn have in context: collocations, structures, and nuances matter.
